# Chapter 7 and 8 figures

## Figure 7.1  (fig-07-01.png, page p-206)

**Kind.** net fragment

**What the text says it models.** Noe's exclusive-OR transition. "The exclusive-OR transition can fire if and only if exactly one of its inputs has tokens and all the others have zero tokens." When it fires "it removes a token only from the input with tokens."

**Structure, briefly.** Two input places p_i and p_k, one transition t_j marked with a circled plus, three output arcs that end in open space (no output places drawn). No marking.

**Placement observations.**
1. The inputs sit left, the outputs fan out right, and t_j is a vertical bar in the middle. *Says:* flow runs left to right, through the bar. *R: in a fragment that shows one transition, inputs go on the entry side and outputs on the exit side, and the bar stands across that axis.*
2. p_i and p_k sit at mirrored heights above and below the bar's center, and their arcs converge on the bar from symmetric angles. *Says:* the two inputs have the same role; neither is primary. That fits the XOR meaning, where the inputs are interchangeable alternatives. *R: places with the same role toward one transition sit mirror-symmetric about that transition's axis.*
3. The input arcs land at slightly different points on the bar (upper and lower), and the three outputs leave from three points spread along the bar. *Says:* each arc has its own attachment; the bar is long enough to hold them apart. *R: a bar's length scales with the larger of its fan-in and fan-out so each arc gets its own attachment point.*
4. The modifier glyph (circled plus) sits on the input side of the bar, at its center, and the label t_j sits below the bar. *Says:* the glyph qualifies how inputs are combined, so it sits where the inputs arrive. *R: a transition-type marker that concerns inputs sits on the bar's input face; the name goes off the end of the bar, clear of the arcs.*
5. Output arcs end in arrowheads with no target. *Says:* "whatever comes next"; the fragment is about the firing rule, not the context. *R: a fragment may show dangling output arcs to mark that the context continues; they fan out evenly.*

**What could be better.** The three outputs are generic; one would do. Otherwise clean and minimal.

## Figure 7.2  (fig-07-02.png, page p-207)

**Kind.** net fragment (two before/after pairs)

**What the text says it models.** Baer's switch. "A switch is a special transition with a special input called the switch input and exactly two outputs labeled e and f." It puts a token in e "if the switch input is empty" or in f "if the switch input is full". The box-shaped place is the switch input.

**Structure, briefly.** One ordinary input place (top-left), one switch input (pentagon, bottom-left), one transition, two output places (f top-right, e bottom-right). Four snapshots: (a) empty switch, before and after; (b) full switch, before and after.

**Placement observations.**
1. Each snapshot has the same layout: inputs left in a column, outputs right in a column, a vertical bar between. Before is left, after is right, with a hollow double arrow between. *Says:* "this state becomes that state"; identical geometry lets the eye diff only the tokens. *R: when showing a firing sequence, repeat the net with identical geometry side by side in time order, so only the marking changes.*
2. The two cases (a) and (b) are stacked vertically, each with its own caption letter centered below. *Says:* rows are cases, columns are time. *R: in a grid of snapshots, one axis is time and the other is alternative cases; never mix them.*
3. The f output is top-right and the e output bottom-right. The ordinary input is top-left and the switch input bottom-left. The switch input's arc meets the bar near its lower end, the e end. *Unclear whether deliberate:* this pairs the switch input visually with e, though e is the output taken when the switch is empty. *R candidate: a special control input enters the bar at one end, not the middle, so it reads as a modifier rather than a peer of the main input.*
4. The output labels f and e sit just beyond the bar's two ends, top and bottom, next to where each output arc leaves. *Says:* the labels name the ports of the bar, not the places. *R: port labels on a transition sit at the bar end where that port's arc attaches.*
5. The switch input has its own shape (a pentagon pointing at the bar), not a circle. Its point aims along the arc toward the transition. *Says:* this place is a control, not a resource. *R: a place with a special role gets a distinct glyph, oriented toward the transition it controls.*
6. Tokens are single dots at the center of each place, including the pentagon. *R: a single token sits at the place's center.*

**What could be better.** Nothing in the layout says which output goes with which switch state except the tiny e and f labels; the reader must match labels to the caption. Placing the switch input nearer the f end (since full means f) might have helped, but that is a guess about intent.

## Figure 7.5  (fig-07-05.png, page p-212)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Kosaraju's priority shared-channel problem (introduced on p-210 with Figure 7.4). Producer P1 fills buffer B1 for consumer C1, producer P2 fills B2 for C2, and "the channel is never to transmit items from buffer B2 to consumer C2 as long as buffer B1 is nonempty." In this net "transition c2 can fire only if there is a token in b2 and p4 and zero tokens in b1."

**Structure, briefly.** Four one-place, one-transition self-loops, each marked: producer 1 (p1, d1), consumer 1 (p2, c1), producer 2 (p3, d2), consumer 2 (p4, c2). Two buffer places b1 (d1 to c1) and b2 (d2 to c2). One inhibitor arc from b1 to c2.

**Placement observations.**
1. The four processes sit at the four corners: producers left, consumers right, pair 1 on the top row, pair 2 on the bottom row. *Says:* the grid encodes two independent facts at once. Columns are roles (who makes, who takes) and rows are the pairs (which items belong together). *R: when processes vary along two dimensions (role and pairing), give each dimension its own axis.*
2. Each process is a marked place directly above a horizontal bar, closed by a near-circular loop on the outer side: the producers' loops swing out left, the consumers' loops swing out right. *Says:* a one-step cyclic process is a little wheel; the loop on the outside keeps the inner side free for the buffers. *R: a one-place self-loop is drawn as a tight circle running place to bar and back, on the side away from the net's center.*
3. Buffers b1 and b2 sit in a middle column between producers and consumers. b1 sits at the height between d1 and c1, so d1 to b1 to c1 reads as a left-to-right handoff along the top row. *Says:* the buffer belongs to neither side; it is the coupling. *R: a buffer place sits between its writer and reader, in the column between the two roles.*
4. b2 is not on the bottom row. It is pulled up to mid-height, just under b1, so d2's output arc climbs a long way up to it and then drops again to c2. *Says:* b1 and b2 are compared by the priority rule, and stacking them next to each other makes them read as a pair (the channel's two queues). The cost is that pair 2's handoff no longer reads as a row. *R candidate: places that a rule compares sit adjacent, even at the cost of bending their own flows.* I am inferring the intent; the text does not say.
5. The inhibitor arc runs from b1 almost straight down to c2, parallel to and beside b2's arc into c2, ending in a small circle at the center of c2's bar. b2's normal arc lands left of it, p4's loop arc lands right of it. *Says:* c2 waits on b2 and p4 but is blocked by b1; the inhibitor is the middle input, flanked by the ordinary ones. *R: an inhibitor arc is drawn like a normal input arc, with its own circle head, and meets the bar between the ordinary inputs rather than sharing their side.* The central slot is weak evidence; it may just be where the arc landed.
6. The inhibitor arc is the longest arc in the figure and the only one that crosses rows. *Says:* this is the one link between the two pairs, the priority, and its length makes it stand out. *R: a cross-group control arc is allowed to be long and visible; it is the feature the figure is about.*
7. Labels sit on the free side. Place labels p1 to p4 sit upper-right of their circles; b1 and b2 labels sit left. Transition labels d1, d2, c1 sit right of the bar; c2's sits left because its right side carries the loop. *R: a label goes on the side of its node that no arc uses.*
8. Tokens are single dots at the center of each process place; buffers are empty. *Says:* each process is ready, nothing has been produced yet. *R: the initially marked places are the process anchors, each at the top of its process.*

**What could be better.** The shared channel itself has no place, so the reader cannot see from placement that c1 and c2 compete for one channel; only the inhibitor carries that. b2's displacement makes pair 2 harder to read than pair 1; the two pairs are not drawn alike, though their structure is identical except for the inhibitor.

## Figure 7.6  (fig-07-06.png, page p-213)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** An abstract example for the constraint-to-inhibitor construction. "If we impose the constraint {p3, p7} (that is, no marking should have p3 and p7 simultaneously marked), then the equivalent Petri net with inhibitor arcs is shown in Figure 7.7." The net models nothing concrete.

**Structure, briefly.** Seven places, six transitions; p1 and p2 marked. t1 takes p3 and p5, gives p2 and p1. t2 takes p3, gives p1 and p4. t3 takes p1 and p4, gives p6. t4 takes p2 and p1, gives p6 and p7. t5 takes p6, gives p3. t6 takes p7, gives p5. So p3 is a choice between t1 and t2, p1 a choice between t3 and t4, and there are two cycles closing back to the left: a lower one through t5 and an upper one through p7, t6, p5.

**Placement observations.**
1. The main flow runs left to right in three columns: p3 on the left; then the bars t1 and t2 stacked; then p2, p1, p4 stacked; then t4 and t3; then p6 on the right. *Says:* tokens move rightward through stages. *R: the main body of an acyclic-looking net is laid out in alternating place and transition columns in flow order.*
2. Bars in the main body are vertical, across the left-to-right flow. t5 on the bottom return is also vertical, across a return arc that runs right to left. t6 on the upper return is tilted, perpendicular to the slanted arc it sits on. *Says:* the bar always crosses its own local arc direction. *R: a bar is perpendicular to the arc through it, even when that arc is diagonal.*
3. The marked places p1 and p2 sit at the center, not at the left. The nominal start p3 is unmarked. *Says:* nothing tells the reader where activity starts; the layout follows structure, not the initial marking. *R violated:* see the critique below.
4. The lower cycle returns from p6 down and around the bottom, through t5, into p3 from below-right. It swings well below p4. The upper cycle leaves t4's top end, rises in a large arc over the whole net into p7 at top-left, then comes down through t6 and p5 into t1. *Says:* both returns run around the outside, one below and one above, so the interior stays the forward flow. *R: return arcs run around the outside of the forward body, split above and below when there are two.*
5. The choice at p3 is drawn as two arcs that diverge up-right to t1 and down-right to t2, symmetric about p3's horizontal. The choice at p1 sends one arc up-right to t4 and one nearly flat to t3. *Says:* a fork of arcs out of a place reads as "one of these". *R: a place in conflict sends its arcs to transitions placed on either side of its own axis, spread apart.*
6. Synchronizing transitions (t1 with p5 and p3; t3 with p1 and p4; t4 with p2 and p1) take their inputs at separate points along the bar, arriving from different sides (above-left and below-left). *R: a joining bar is long enough that each input lands at its own point, ordered along the bar as the input places are ordered in space.*
7. Labels: place labels sit at an outer diagonal (p3 lower-left, p6 lower-right, p5 lower-left, p7 upper-left, p2 upper-right, p1 below, p4 lower-right). Transition labels sit at a bar end (t1, t2, t3 above; t4, t5 below; t6 to the right of a tilted bar). *R: labels go at the node's least crowded side.*
8. The constraint {p3, p7} is not drawn at all; it lives only in the caption. p3 and p7 both sit on the left edge, one middle, one top. *Unclear whether that is meant to suggest the pair.* *R candidate: elements tied by an undrawn relation sit near each other so the caption's claim can be found.*

**What could be better.** The marked places are buried in the middle; a reader cannot find the start. The upper return arc is very large and the column through p7, t6, p5 runs down-right at a slant, which looks improvised rather than planned. There is no visible device for the constraint.

## Figure 7.7  (fig-07-07.png, page p-214)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "A Petri net with inhibitor arcs corresponding to the Petri net with constraints of Figure 7.6. The inhibitor arcs assure that tokens cannot simultaneously be in places p3 and p7." By the construction on p-213, every transition that outputs to one constraint member gets an inhibitor arc from the other member: t4 (which outputs p7) is inhibited by p3, and t5 (which outputs p3) is inhibited by p7.

**Structure, briefly.** Figure 7.6 plus two inhibitor arcs: p3 to t4 and p7 to t5.

**Placement observations.**
1. Every node of 7.6 is in the same relative position. The two figures can be compared by overlay. *Says:* "this is the same net, with something added". *R: a net derived from another keeps the source's placement exactly; only the added elements are new.*
2. The inhibitor p7 to t5 leaves p7 at top-left, sweeps down the far left outside the whole net, along the bottom, and ends at the bottom end of t5's bar with a small circle. It is the outermost curve in the drawing. *Says:* it is an add-on that wraps around the existing net rather than through it. *R: an arc added to an existing layout routes around the outside when the direct line would cross the body.*
3. The inhibitor p3 to t4 leaves p3 upward, arcs over p5's arc into t1 and over p2, and lands on the top end of t4's bar. It crosses the p5 to t1 arc. *Says:* this one goes through the interior, crossing an arc, to keep its length moderate. The two inhibitors are routed by different policies.
4. Both inhibitors attach at a bar end (t4's top, t5's bottom), not the bar's middle. The ordinary arcs keep their old attachment points. *Says:* the inhibitor is an extra input bolted onto the existing port arrangement. *R: a newly added input attaches at the free end of the bar so existing attachments do not move.* In 7.5 the inhibitor met the middle; so the position on the bar is not a stable convention.
5. Each inhibitor reaches the bar from the side of the bar opposite to where it would naturally arrive: t5's inhibitor comes in from below-left to a bar whose normal input arrives from the right. *Says:* little; it is where room was.
6. Labels, tokens, and bar orientations are unchanged from 7.6.

**What could be better.** The two inhibitor arcs are the point of the figure, and the constraint is symmetric (p3 and p7 inhibit each other's producers), but the two arcs are drawn unlike each other: one wraps the far outside, the other cuts through the interior. A reader cannot see the symmetry. Placing p3 and p7 closer together in 7.6 would have let both inhibitors be short and mirror images. The base layout was not chosen with this extension in mind, and keeping it unchanged costs legibility here.

## Figure 7.8  (fig-07-08.png, page p-215)

**Kind.** net fragment (before and after a transformation)

**What the text says it models.** Splitting a transition t_j into a start transition t_j' and an end transition t_j'' with a place p_j between, where "the place p_j' represents the firing of t_j." This is used to turn inhibitor arcs into constraints.

**Structure, briefly.** Top: one transition with three dangling inputs labeled I(t_j) and two dangling outputs labeled O(t_j). Bottom: t_j' with the same three inputs, one arc to p_j, one arc to t_j'' with the same two outputs.

**Placement observations.**
1. The original is on top, the expansion below, and both span the same horizontal range: inputs entering from the left edge, outputs leaving at the right edge. The set labels I(t_j) and O(t_j) sit at the same x in both. *Says:* the outside world is unchanged; only the middle is opened up. *R: in a before/after pair, the interface (inputs and outputs) stays at the same positions; the rewritten part fills the space between them.*
2. The expansion is a straight horizontal line: bar, place, bar, all on the center axis, with straight arcs between. *Says:* "start, in progress, end" is a sequence in time. *R: a chain introduced by refinement lies on one straight line along the flow axis.*
3. Bars are vertical across the horizontal flow. Transition labels sit above their bars; the place label p_j sits below its circle. *R: transition names above the bar end, place names below the circle, when the flow is horizontal and both sides are free.*
4. Input arcs fan in from above-left, left, and below-left to separate points on the bar. Outputs fan out from the bar's top and bottom. *R: a bundle of dangling arcs stands for "a set of places"; it is labeled once, at the bundle's open end, by a set name.*

**What could be better.** Clean. The vertical gap between the two parts is large, but it separates them clearly.

## Figure 7.9  (fig-07-09.png, page p-215)

**Kind.** net fragment (before and after a transformation)

**What the text says it models.** An exclusive-OR transition "is equivalent to a set of transitions, one for each element in I(t_j). Each transition has one (normal) input, and the rest of the inputs are inhibitor arcs."

**Structure, briefly.** (a) Two places into one XOR transition, one output arc to O(t_j). (b) The same two places; two transitions stacked vertically at the bar position. Each place has a normal arc to the transition at its own height and an inhibitor arc to the other transition. Both transitions output to O(t_j).

**Placement observations.**
1. (a) is above (b). The two input places sit at identical positions in both parts; the single bar becomes two bars at the same x; the output label O(t_j) stays to the right. *Says:* inputs and outputs are fixed; the transition was replaced in place. *R: when one node is replaced by several, the replacements occupy the original node's position, spread along the axis across the flow.*
2. In (b), each place's normal arc runs nearly flat to the bar at its own height (top place to top bar, bottom place to bottom bar). The two inhibitor arcs cross diagonally in an X between the columns. *Says:* the straight lines are "this one fires for me"; the X is "and not the other". The crossing is the meaning. *R: a pair of mutual exclusions between two rows is drawn as a symmetric X of inhibitor arcs between straight row arcs.*
3. Normal arcs attach at the outer end of each bar (top of the upper bar, bottom of the lower bar). Inhibitor arcs attach at the inner ends, facing each other across the gap. *Says:* the inhibitors form an inner pair, the normal inputs an outer pair. *R: on a bar with both kinds of input, ordinary arcs land on the end nearest their source and inhibitor arcs land on the other end, so each kind groups.*
4. The two output arcs converge to a single point at O(t_j). *Says:* both alternatives give the same result. *R: arcs that lead to the same target set converge to one labeled point.*
5. The whole of (b) is mirror-symmetric about the horizontal center line. *R: interchangeable branches are drawn as mirror images.*

**What could be better.** Nothing serious. The figure shows only two inputs; with three, the X would become a tangle, and the drawing does not suggest how it scales.

## Figure 7.10  (fig-07-10.png, page p-216)

**Kind.** net fragment (before and after a transformation)

**What the text says it models.** "Switches can also be easily transformed into inhibitor arcs." (a) is a switch, (b) the equivalent net with inhibitor arcs.

**Structure, briefly.** (a) An ordinary input (top-left), a switch input (pentagon, bottom-left), one transition with ports e (top) and f (bottom), two outputs right. (b) Two transitions stacked at the bar's x. The upper one, e, takes the top place and is inhibited by the bottom (switch) place; it outputs top-right. The lower one, f, takes both places as normal inputs; it outputs bottom-right.

**Placement observations.**
1. As in 7.9, the four outer places keep their positions from (a) to (b); the single bar becomes two bars at the same x. *R: replacement nodes occupy the original node's position, spread across the flow.*
2. In (b) each output place sits on the same row as the transition that feeds it, with a perfectly horizontal arc. The upper-left place also feeds the upper bar horizontally; the lower-left place feeds the lower bar horizontally. *Says:* each row is one outcome: "switch empty, go here" on top, "switch full, go there" below. *R: when a choice is split into one transition per outcome, each outcome is a straight row from inputs to its output.*
3. The cross-row arcs (switch place to e as inhibitor, top place to f as normal) form an X between the left column and the bars, as in 7.9. Here one arm is an inhibitor and the other a normal arc, so the X is not symmetric in meaning, though it is symmetric in shape.
4. Port labels e and f move. In (a) e is above the bar and f below. In (b) e sits below the upper bar and f above the lower bar, at the inner ends, beside where the crossing arcs land. Note also that 7.2 placed f on top and e below; 7.10 reverses it. *Says:* the port-label position is not fixed by convention; it follows wherever there is room. *R: a port label goes next to the attachment point it names.*
5. The switch place loses its pentagon shape in (b) and becomes a circle, because in (b) it is an ordinary place.

**What could be better.** The swap of e and f between 7.2 and 7.10 forces the reader to re-learn which end is which. A layout engine should fix one convention for ordered ports.

## Figure 7.11  (fig-07-11.png, page p-217)

**Kind.** net fragment

**What the text says it models.** Testing a place for zero with priorities. "If we put a token into place p = 0? and define the priority of transition t1 to be higher than the priority of transition t2, then we will get a token in one of the two places at the right," "Place p is empty" or "Place p is not empty".

**Structure, briefly.** A request place "p = 0?" in conflict between t2 and t1. t1 also has a read loop on p (an arc in and an arc back out). t2 outputs to "Place p is empty", t1 to "Place p is not empty". No marking.

**Placement observations.**
1. The request place is at the left, mid-height. Its two arcs fan out to t2 up-right and t1 down-right. The outputs sit to the right on the same rows as their transitions, joined by horizontal arcs. *Says:* "ask, then one of two answers", read left to right. *R: a choice fans from its place into rows, one row per alternative, each row ending at its own result.*
2. Output places carry prose labels to their right ("Place p is empty", "Place p is not empty") instead of symbols. *Says:* the outcome's meaning is the label. *R: a result place at the end of the flow carries its label on the far (exit) side, where the flow would continue.*
3. The tested place p sits below-left of t1, close in, connected by two short parallel horizontal arcs, one to the bar and one back. *Says:* p is read, not consumed; it is a side condition of t1 rather than part of the main flow. *R: a place that a transition only tests (arc in and out) sits close beside that transition, off the main axis, joined by a short parallel pair of arcs.*
4. Bars are vertical, labels below each bar (t2, t1). The request label "p = 0?" sits above its place, p's label below-left.
5. The higher-priority transition t1 is placed lower, t2 higher. Priority has no spatial expression. *R candidate (not followed here): priority order maps to reading order (higher priority first).*

**What could be better.** Priority, the point of the figure, is invisible; it lives in the caption. Numbering t2 above t1 reads backward. Placing t1 on top would have let the reading order carry the priority.

## Figure 7.12  (fig-07-12.png, page p-219)

**Kind.** net fragment (three instruction templates)

**What the text says it models.** Register machine instructions as extended-net transitions. Registers are places p_1', ..., p_n'; program-counter positions are places p_i. "(a) P(n): Increase register n by 1. (b) D(n): Decrease register n by 1 (register n must be nonzero). (c) J(n)[s]: Jump to statement s if register n is zero."

**Structure, briefly.** (a) p_i to bar to p_(i+1), bar also outputs to p_n'. (b) p_i and p_n' into bar, bar to p_(i+1). (c) p_i in conflict between a left bar (inhibited by p_n', outputs p_s) and a right bar (reads p_n' with an arc in and back, outputs p_(i+1)).

**Placement observations.**
1. In (a) and (b) the control flow is a vertical column: p_i on top, horizontal bar, p_(i+1) below, straight arcs. *Says:* the program counter moves down one step, like reading a program listing. *R: the control thread of a sequential program is a vertical column read top to bottom.*
2. The register p_n' sits to the right of the column in every part. *Says:* data lives beside the control flow, not in it. *R: places that hold data (counters, registers, resources) sit to one consistent side of the control column, the same side throughout a family of figures.*
3. The register's height encodes direction. In (a), where the instruction adds to it, p_n' sits low, between the bar and p_(i+1), and the arc leaves the bar's right end, drops, and turns right in a rounded right angle. In (b), where the instruction takes from it, p_n' sits high, level with p_i, and its arc curves down into the bar's right end. *Says:* inputs come from above, outputs go below; the register sits at the level of the other things the bar takes or gives. *R: a side place sits level with the bar's other inputs if it is an input, level with the outputs if it is an output.*
4. (a) and (b) sit side by side with the column at the same height and spacing, so they read as a pair of templates. *R: templates of the same family share one skeleton geometry.*
5. In (c), p_i sits on top, centered over two bars on one row. The arcs to them curve symmetrically left and right. The jump target p_s is under the left bar, the fall-through p_(i+1) under the right bar. *Says:* a two-way branch; both outcomes are one step down. *R: a two-way branch places its place centered above the two transitions, which sit side by side on one row; each outcome sits straight below its transition.*
6. The fall-through is on the right, the register's side. The jump is on the left, away from it. The inhibitor arc from p_n' to the left bar therefore crosses the whole figure and crosses p_i's right arc. The read loop from p_n' to the right bar is a tight two-arc loop, one arc in and one curving back out from below. *Says:* the right transition "uses" the register closely; the left one is gated from afar. The crossing is a cost.
7. The inhibitor arc ends in a small circle on the bar's right end, beside where p_i's arc lands. *R: an inhibitor lands at the bar end nearest its source.*
8. Labels: place labels sit left of the circle for column places in (a) and (b), right of p_n' and right of p_i in (c). Transitions are unlabeled. Labels sit on the side away from the side place.

**What could be better.** In (c), putting the jump branch on the register's side would have made the inhibitor short and avoided the crossing; but then the fall-through would be off to the left, breaking the habit of "next" being straight on. The drawing keeps fall-through near its (a)/(b) position at the cost of the inhibitor. Neither choice is clearly best.

## Figure 7.13  (fig-07-13.png, page p-222)

**Kind.** net (marked graph)

**What the text says it models.** An abstract marked graph, a net "in which each place is an input for exactly one transition and an output of exactly one transition." The text uses it to show cycles: "t1 t2 t1 is a cycle, as are t4 t3 t4 and t2 t4 t3 t1 t2." The theorems that follow are about the token count on each cycle (liveness iff every cycle has at least one token).

**Structure, briefly.** Six places, four transitions. Left cycle: t1, p2, t2, p1. Right cycle: t3, p6, t4, p5. Joining places p3 (t2 to t4, across the top) and p4 (t3 to t1, across the bottom). p2 and p6 marked. The third cycle t2, p3, t4, p5, t3, p4, t1, p2 runs through both joins.

**Placement observations.**
1. Each two-transition cycle is drawn as a literal circle: transitions at top and bottom, places at left and right, arcs as quarter-circle curves. *Says:* "this is a cycle". The shape is the claim. Since the following theorems are all about cycles and their token counts, the reader can check them by eye: one token on each circle. *R: a small cycle is drawn as a circle, its nodes evenly spaced on the circumference.*
2. The two circles are the same size, at the same height, side by side, with the same rotation sense (both run bottom, right, top, left: counterclockwise). The marked place is in the same position (the right side) in both. *Says:* the two cycles are the same kind of thing, peers. *R: repeated substructures are drawn as translated copies, same orientation, same rotation sense, marking in the corresponding spot.* Note: translated, not mirrored. Mirroring would have reversed one cycle's direction.
3. The joining places sit on the vertical center line between the circles: p3 at the top, level with the transitions it joins (t2 and t4), p4 at the bottom, level with t1 and t3. Their arcs are long shallow curves. *Says:* p3 and p4 belong to neither cycle; they are the coupling, one each way. *R: a place that couples two cycles sits midway between them, at the height of the transitions it connects.*
4. The joins connect the tops of the two circles and the bottoms. Data goes left to right across the top and right to left across the bottom, so the whole figure also reads as a large clockwise loop. *Says:* the two cycles are linked in a ring. *R: two-way coupling between side-by-side groups uses the top for one direction and the bottom for the other.*
5. Bars are vertical. At the top and bottom of each circle the cycle's arcs arrive horizontally, so the bar is perpendicular to the path. The joining arcs also arrive horizontally. *R: a bar is perpendicular to the path through it.*

7. The third cycle (through p3, p5, p4, p2) is not drawn as a shape of its own. It follows the inner halves of the two circles and the two joins, so it reads as a lens in the middle. A reader can trace it but cannot see it at a glance.

**What could be better.** The design favors the two small cycles. The big cycle, which the text lists and which determines liveness just as much, has to be traced. Also, the marked places sit on the inner side of their circles (p2) and the outer side (p6), so the token on the big cycle (p2) is not visually distinguished from the one off it (p6). Otherwise this is one of the clearest figures in the chapter: symmetric, uncluttered, with shape tied directly to the concept under discussion.
