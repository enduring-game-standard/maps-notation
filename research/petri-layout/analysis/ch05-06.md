# Chapters 5 and 6: placement analysis

## Figure 5.2  (fig-05-02.png, page p-138)

**Kind.** net fragment

**What the text says it models.** A reduction of submarking reachability to zero reachability. Each original place p_i not in P' gets a new drain transition t_i' "with input {p_i} and null output". Each p_i in P' gets a new place p_i' holding μ'(p_i) tokens and a transition t_i' "with input {p_i, p_i'} and null output". "Figure 5.2 illustrates the two types of transitions introduced."

**Structure, briefly.** An amorphous blob labeled "Original Petri net" stands for the whole given net. Two representative places sit inside it near its right edge. Top case: p_i to a sink transition t_i'. Bottom case: p_i and a new place p_i' (marking written inside as "μ(p_i)") both feed one sink transition t_i'. No outputs.

**Placement observations.**
1. The original net is a freeform closed outline, not a rectangle, filling the left two thirds. Its label sits inside, upper left, away from the parts that matter. *Says:* "this is some arbitrary net you do not need to see." *R: an abstracted subnet is drawn as an enclosing outline with its name inside, in the region free of the drawn detail.*
2. The two representative places sit inside the outline, close to its right boundary. The added transitions sit outside. Arcs cross the boundary. *Says:* old stuff in, new stuff out; the boundary is the line between given and constructed. *R: in a construction, the given net's elements stay inside its outline and the added elements go outside, on the side facing them; only arcs cross the outline.*
3. The two cases are stacked vertically, each one a horizontal row, left to right: original place, transition, (new place). *Says:* two independent per-place cases, read as two separate sentences. *R: alternative construction cases are separate rows stacked vertically, each read left to right.*
4. Bottom case: t_i' sits between p_i and p_i', with arcs entering from both sides (left and right). The new place is to the right of the bar, mirroring the old place on the left. *Says:* the transition synchronizes old and new, it consumes from both. *R: a transition with two inputs from different sources sits between them, inputs entering from opposite sides.*
5. The bars are vertical across horizontal flow. The bottom bar sits almost on the outline (the outline bulges right at that height and the bar is just beyond it); the top bar is far out. Unclear whether the different distances mean anything; I read it as slack.
6. Labels go below each node (p_i ∉ P', t_i', p_i'), with set membership written into the place label. The initial marking is written as a symbol inside the circle, "μ(p_i)", not as dots. *R: a symbolic marking goes inside the place; the name goes below.*
7. Arcs are straight and horizontal.

**What could be better.** The top transition is far from its place with empty space between; the two t_i' bars do not line up vertically, so the cases look less parallel than they are. Same label t_i' on both rows is a notational choice, but alignment would show they are the same role.

## Figure 5.4  (fig-05-04.png, page p-142)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "Every place in the general Petri net is replaced by a ring of places in the restricted Petri net." Tokens "can freely move around the ring to any position", so a transition needing three tokens from p_i can take one from each of three ring places. Ring length k_i is a parameter.

**Structure, briefly.** One simple cycle p_i,1 → p_i,2 → p_i,3 → p_i,4 → ... → p_i,k_i → p_i,1, one unlabeled transition between each pair. No marking. The segment between p_i,4 and p_i,k_i is elided with dots.

**Placement observations.**
1. The places are the vertices of a regular pentagon, p_i,1 at the apex, numbering clockwise. *Says:* this is a ring with no start and no end; any position is equivalent. A pure cycle with no distinguished entry is drawn as a closed polygon, not cut open into a line (contrast the producer-consumer columns of 3.29, which are cut at the marked place). *R: a cycle with no marked or privileged place, whose point is symmetry, is drawn as a regular polygon; places at the vertices, transitions at edge midpoints.*
2. Index 1 is at top center; numbering runs clockwise. *Says:* reading starts at 12 o'clock like a clock face. *R: when a ring has an index origin, put it at the top and number clockwise.*
3. Transitions are short bars at the midpoint of each edge, perpendicular to the edge, so they rotate with it (diagonal bars on diagonal edges, a vertical bar on the horizontal bottom edge). *R: a bar is perpendicular to the arc it interrupts, whatever that arc's angle.*
4. The parameterized length is shown by breaking one edge (lower left, from p_i,4 to p_i,k_i) with a dotted gap, an arrowhead on each side of the gap and no transition drawn there. *Says:* "more of the same goes here". The last named place, p_i,k_i, sits next to p_i,1 so the wrap-around edge is drawn in full. *R: an elided run of a repeated chain is a dotted gap in one edge; draw the first and last elements and the closing edge in full.*
5. Labels sit outside the ring, each beside its place on the outward side (above the apex, right of right-side places, left of left-side places). The interior is empty. *R: on a ring, labels go on the outside, radially.*
6. Arcs are straight edges of the polygon.

**What could be better.** The pentagon reads as exactly five places at first glance; the dotted gap is small, so k_i = 5 is the default impression. The transitions are unlabeled, which is fine here since they are anonymous.

## Figure 5.6  (fig-05-06.png, page p-146)

**Kind.** net fragment

**What the text says it models.** Converts "is p_i ever zero?" into "is this net live?". Added: a "run" place r_1 (marked) that every original transition t_j uses as input and output; a sink s_1 that removes the run token and "freezes" the net; s_2 from p_i to r_2; and s_3, which "floods" the net, input r_2, output every place (all p_i, r_1, r_2).

**Structure, briefly.** A blob "Petri net C_1" holding representative t_j and places p_1, p_2, ..., p_i, p_n. Outside: r_1 (one token) with a self-loop pair to t_j and an arc to s_1; s_2 between p_i and r_2; s_3 as a long horizontal bar fanning out to all places and back to r_1, plus a self-loop with r_2.

**Placement observations.**
1. The original net is again a freeform outline; added nodes sit outside it: r_1 and s_1 top right, s_2, r_2, s_3 at the bottom. *Says:* the boundary separates given from constructed. *R (same as 5.2): given elements inside the outline, added elements outside, arcs cross.* But t_j sits right on the boundary at top, its bar half outside. Unclear whether that is intended.
2. The "run" control (r_1, s_1, t_j) is grouped in a top row, read left to right, separate from the "test and flood" machinery at the bottom. *Says:* two mechanisms, two regions. *R: each added mechanism of a construction gets its own region around the subject net.*
3. r_1's self-loop with t_j is drawn as two parallel slightly bowed arcs, one each direction, between the bar and the place. *Says:* "uses r_1 and gives it back", a read-arc. *R: a self-loop between a place and a transition is two arcs bowed apart into a lens, one per direction.* The r_2/s_3 self-loop is drawn differently: a small tight loop beside the bar end. Two styles for one concept in one figure.
4. s_3 is a long horizontal bar at the bottom. Its many output arcs leave from points spread along the bar and fan upward in curves to each place. *Says:* one transition, many outputs: the "flood". The long bar gives each output its own departure point. *R: a transition with many outputs is a long bar; outputs leave from distinct points along it, ordered to match their targets left to right.*
5. The return from s_3 to r_1 swings below everything and up the right side as one large curve, outside the blob. *Says:* the reset loop closes around the whole net. *R: a return arc that spans the construction runs around the outside of the subject outline.*
6. Elision: a dotted line between the fan arcs (between p_2's and p_i's arcs) says "one arc per place". *R: a fan to an arbitrary set shows two or three representative arcs and dots between them.*
7. p_i, s_2, r_2 form a straight vertical column downward. *Says:* the test chain is one straight sequence. Bar s_2 is horizontal across the vertical flow.
8. Labels to the right of places and at the bar end for transitions; t_j and s_1 labels below their vertical bars. Token as a dot in r_1.

**What could be better.** This is crowded and weak. The fan arcs from s_3 cross the blob boundary at a sharp angle and pass close to each other, and p_i overlaps the blob's lower boundary, so it is hard to tell whether p_i is inside. The t_j self-loop also crosses the boundary twice. The top-right group and the bottom group are tied only by a very long arc; a reader has to trace it.

## Figure 5.7  (fig-05-07.png, page p-148)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** A small example for t_j-dead markings: "(2,0), (1,0), (0,0), (0,1), (0,2), ... are t_2-dead". The net exists to have multiplicities that make t_2 hard to enable.

**Structure, briefly.** Two places p_1 (top) and p_2 (bottom), no marking drawn. t_1 takes two tokens from p_1 and puts one in p_2. t_2 connects to both places with multiple arcs in each direction. As far as I can read, between p_1 and t_2 there is one arc into t_2 and three back into p_1; between p_2 and t_2 two arcs into t_2 and one back to p_2.

**Placement observations.**
1. p_1 above p_2 in a left column; t_1 between them on the column as a horizontal bar. *Says:* the simple flow p_1 to p_2 runs downward. *R: a straight chain runs top to bottom with the bar across it.*
2. t_2 sits to the right as a tall vertical bar, at the midheight between p_1 and p_2, forming a triangle with them. *Says:* t_2 relates to both places equally; it is not in the main column. *R: a transition connected to two places of a column sits off to the side at the height between them, its bar parallel to the column so both groups of arcs land on its face.*
3. Arc multiplicity is drawn as literally repeated parallel arcs, offset side by side, with arrowheads showing direction per arc. Two arcs from p_1 to t_1 enter the bar at two points. *Says:* "two tokens". *R: weight k is k parallel straight arcs, evenly offset; input and output arcs between the same pair share the bundle, each with its own head.* (Peterson elsewhere uses this convention; here it is the point of the figure.)
4. Labels left of places and left of t_1, right of t_2: all on the outside, away from the arcs.

**What could be better.** The bundles of 4 and 3 parallel arcs, mixed in direction, are hard to count and harder to sort by direction; you must find each arrowhead. Grouping same-direction arcs together, or a numeric weight, would read faster. No marking is drawn, which is fine as the text varies it.

## Figure 5.9  (fig-05-09.png, page p-152)

**Kind.** net fragment

**What the text says it models.** "The general scheme" for a net that weakly computes a polynomial Q. "The input values x_1, ..., x_n are encoded by x_i tokens in p_i", "Initially a token also resides in the 'run' place", and when done "the 'output' place will have y tokens, where y ≤ Q(x_1, ..., x_n)".

**Structure, briefly.** A rounded rectangle Q (the subnet, contents hidden). Interface places outside it: inputs p_1 ... p_n on the left, Output on the right, Run and Quit below. Arcs stop inside the box without reaching any visible node.

**Placement observations.**
1. The subnet is a rounded rectangle with only its name inside (top right). Arcs enter and stop with an arrowhead in empty interior space. *Says:* "this connects to something inside; you do not need to know what." *R: a black-box subnet is a rounded rectangle; arcs to hidden internals penetrate the border a short way and end with an arrowhead in empty space.*
2. Data inputs in a vertical column on the left, data output on the right, at roughly the height of the second input. *Says:* data flows left to right through Q, like a function box. *R: a computing subnet's data inputs line its left side, its result places sit on its right side.*
3. Control places (Run in, Quit out) go below the box, Run to the left of Quit. *Says:* control is a different axis from data. Start before end in reading order. *R: control places (start/stop) of a subnet sit on a side perpendicular to the data flow, entry before exit in reading order.*
4. The input column is a repeated element: p_1, p_2, vertical dots, p_n, evenly spaced, all the same size, with arcs entering at matching heights. *R: an indexed family of places is a column of identical nodes, with vertical ellipsis between the second and last.*
5. Place names here go outside to the left (p_1) while the symbolic marking (x_1) goes inside the circle. For Output, Run, Quit, the role name goes below. *R: symbolic marking inside the place; name outside.*
6. Interface places sit outside the box, not on its border. Arcs are straight; diagonal for Run and Quit.

**What could be better.** The Run and Quit arcs are diagonal for no visible reason; straight vertical would match the clean horizontal inputs. Output is not aligned with any input or with the box center, so its height looks arbitrary.
