# Chapters 5 and 6: placement analysis

## Figure 5.2  (fig-05-02.png, page p-138)

**Kind.** net fragment

**What the text says it models.** A reduction of submarking reachability to zero reachability. Each original place p_i not in P' gets a new drain transition t_i' "with input {p_i} and null output". Each p_i in P' gets a new place p_i' holding μ'(p_i) tokens and a transition t_i' "with input {p_i, p_i'} and null output". "Figure 5.2 illustrates the two types of transitions introduced."

**Structure, briefly.** An amorphous blob labeled "Original Petri net" stands for the whole given net. Two representative places sit inside it near its right edge. Top case: p_i to a sink transition t_i'. Bottom case: p_i and a new place p_i' (marking written inside as "μ(p_i)") both feed one sink transition t_i'. No outputs.

**Placement observations.**
1. The original net is a freeform closed outline, not a rectangle, filling the left two thirds. Its label sits inside, upper left, away from the parts that matter. *Says:* "this is some arbitrary net you do not need to see." *R: an abstracted subnet is drawn as an enclosing outline with its name inside, in the region free of the drawn detail.*
2. The two representative places sit inside the outline, close to its right boundary. The added transitions sit outside. Arcs cross the boundary. *Says:* old stuff in, new stuff out; the boundary is the line between given and constructed. *R: in a construction, the given net's elements stay inside its outline and the added elements go outside, on the side facing them; only arcs cross the outline.*
3. The two cases are stacked vertically, each one a horizontal row, left to right: original place, transition, (new place). *Says:* two independent per-place cases, read as two separate sentences. *R: alternative construction cases are separate rows stacked vertically, each read left to right.*
4. Bottom case: t_i' sits between p_i and p_i', with arcs entering from both sides (left and right). The new place is to the right of the bar, mirroring the old place on the left. *Says:* the transition synchronizes old and new, it consumes from both. *R: a transition with two inputs from different sources sits between them, inputs entering from opposite sides.*
5. The bars are vertical across horizontal flow. The bottom bar sits almost on the outline (the outline bulges right at that height and the bar is just beyond it); the top bar is far out. Unclear whether the different distances mean anything; I read it as slack.
6. Labels go below each node (p_i ∉ P', t_i', p_i'), with set membership written into the place label. The initial marking is written as a symbol inside the circle, "μ(p_i)", not as dots. *R: a symbolic marking goes inside the place; the name goes below.*
7. Arcs are straight and horizontal.

**What could be better.** The top transition is far from its place with empty space between; the two t_i' bars do not line up vertically, so the cases look less parallel than they are. Same label t_i' on both rows is a notational choice, but alignment would show they are the same role.

## Figure 5.4  (fig-05-04.png, page p-142)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "Every place in the general Petri net is replaced by a ring of places in the restricted Petri net." Tokens "can freely move around the ring to any position", so a transition needing three tokens from p_i can take one from each of three ring places. Ring length k_i is a parameter.

**Structure, briefly.** One simple cycle p_i,1 → p_i,2 → p_i,3 → p_i,4 → ... → p_i,k_i → p_i,1, one unlabeled transition between each pair. No marking. The segment between p_i,4 and p_i,k_i is elided with dots.

**Placement observations.**
1. The places are the vertices of a regular pentagon, p_i,1 at the apex, numbering clockwise. *Says:* this is a ring with no start and no end; any position is equivalent. A pure cycle with no distinguished entry is drawn as a closed polygon, not cut open into a line (contrast the producer-consumer columns of 3.29, which are cut at the marked place). *R: a cycle with no marked or privileged place, whose point is symmetry, is drawn as a regular polygon; places at the vertices, transitions at edge midpoints.*
2. Index 1 is at top center; numbering runs clockwise. *Says:* reading starts at 12 o'clock like a clock face. *R: when a ring has an index origin, put it at the top and number clockwise.*
3. Transitions are short bars at the midpoint of each edge, perpendicular to the edge, so they rotate with it (diagonal bars on diagonal edges, a vertical bar on the horizontal bottom edge). *R: a bar is perpendicular to the arc it interrupts, whatever that arc's angle.*
4. The parameterized length is shown by breaking one edge (lower left, from p_i,4 to p_i,k_i) with a dotted gap, an arrowhead on each side of the gap and no transition drawn there. *Says:* "more of the same goes here". The last named place, p_i,k_i, sits next to p_i,1 so the wrap-around edge is drawn in full. *R: an elided run of a repeated chain is a dotted gap in one edge; draw the first and last elements and the closing edge in full.*
5. Labels sit outside the ring, each beside its place on the outward side (above the apex, right of right-side places, left of left-side places). The interior is empty. *R: on a ring, labels go on the outside, radially.*
6. Arcs are straight edges of the polygon.

**What could be better.** The pentagon reads as exactly five places at first glance; the dotted gap is small, so k_i = 5 is the default impression. The transitions are unlabeled, which is fine here since they are anonymous.

## Figure 5.6  (fig-05-06.png, page p-146)

**Kind.** net fragment

**What the text says it models.** Converts "is p_i ever zero?" into "is this net live?". Added: a "run" place r_1 (marked) that every original transition t_j uses as input and output; a sink s_1 that removes the run token and "freezes" the net; s_2 from p_i to r_2; and s_3, which "floods" the net, input r_2, output every place (all p_i, r_1, r_2).

**Structure, briefly.** A blob "Petri net C_1" holding representative t_j and places p_1, p_2, ..., p_i, p_n. Outside: r_1 (one token) with a self-loop pair to t_j and an arc to s_1; s_2 between p_i and r_2; s_3 as a long horizontal bar fanning out to all places and back to r_1, plus a self-loop with r_2.

**Placement observations.**
1. The original net is again a freeform outline; added nodes sit outside it: r_1 and s_1 top right, s_2, r_2, s_3 at the bottom. *Says:* the boundary separates given from constructed. *R (same as 5.2): given elements inside the outline, added elements outside, arcs cross.* But t_j sits right on the boundary at top, its bar half outside. Unclear whether that is intended.
2. The "run" control (r_1, s_1, t_j) is grouped in a top row, read left to right, separate from the "test and flood" machinery at the bottom. *Says:* two mechanisms, two regions. *R: each added mechanism of a construction gets its own region around the subject net.*
3. r_1's self-loop with t_j is drawn as two parallel slightly bowed arcs, one each direction, between the bar and the place. *Says:* "uses r_1 and gives it back", a read-arc. *R: a self-loop between a place and a transition is two arcs bowed apart into a lens, one per direction.* The r_2/s_3 self-loop is drawn differently: a small tight loop beside the bar end. Two styles for one concept in one figure.
4. s_3 is a long horizontal bar at the bottom. Its many output arcs leave from points spread along the bar and fan upward in curves to each place. *Says:* one transition, many outputs: the "flood". The long bar gives each output its own departure point. *R: a transition with many outputs is a long bar; outputs leave from distinct points along it, ordered to match their targets left to right.*
5. The return from s_3 to r_1 swings below everything and up the right side as one large curve, outside the blob. *Says:* the reset loop closes around the whole net. *R: a return arc that spans the construction runs around the outside of the subject outline.*
6. Elision: a dotted line between the fan arcs (between p_2's and p_i's arcs) says "one arc per place". *R: a fan to an arbitrary set shows two or three representative arcs and dots between them.*
7. p_i, s_2, r_2 form a straight vertical column downward. *Says:* the test chain is one straight sequence. Bar s_2 is horizontal across the vertical flow.
8. Labels to the right of places and at the bar end for transitions; t_j and s_1 labels below their vertical bars. Token as a dot in r_1.

**What could be better.** This is crowded and weak. The fan arcs from s_3 cross the blob boundary at a sharp angle and pass close to each other, and p_i overlaps the blob's lower boundary, so it is hard to tell whether p_i is inside. The t_j self-loop also crosses the boundary twice. The top-right group and the bottom group are tied only by a very long arc; a reader has to trace it.

## Figure 5.7  (fig-05-07.png, page p-148)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** A small example for t_j-dead markings: "(2,0), (1,0), (0,0), (0,1), (0,2), ... are t_2-dead". The net exists to have multiplicities that make t_2 hard to enable.

**Structure, briefly.** Two places p_1 (top) and p_2 (bottom), no marking drawn. t_1 takes two tokens from p_1 and puts one in p_2. t_2 connects to both places with multiple arcs in each direction. As far as I can read, between p_1 and t_2 there is one arc into t_2 and three back into p_1; between p_2 and t_2 two arcs into t_2 and one back to p_2.

**Placement observations.**
1. p_1 above p_2 in a left column; t_1 between them on the column as a horizontal bar. *Says:* the simple flow p_1 to p_2 runs downward. *R: a straight chain runs top to bottom with the bar across it.*
2. t_2 sits to the right as a tall vertical bar, at the midheight between p_1 and p_2, forming a triangle with them. *Says:* t_2 relates to both places equally; it is not in the main column. *R: a transition connected to two places of a column sits off to the side at the height between them, its bar parallel to the column so both groups of arcs land on its face.*
3. Arc multiplicity is drawn as literally repeated parallel arcs, offset side by side, with arrowheads showing direction per arc. Two arcs from p_1 to t_1 enter the bar at two points. *Says:* "two tokens". *R: weight k is k parallel straight arcs, evenly offset; input and output arcs between the same pair share the bundle, each with its own head.* (Peterson elsewhere uses this convention; here it is the point of the figure.)
4. Labels left of places and left of t_1, right of t_2: all on the outside, away from the arcs.

**What could be better.** The bundles of 4 and 3 parallel arcs, mixed in direction, are hard to count and harder to sort by direction; you must find each arrowhead. Grouping same-direction arcs together, or a numeric weight, would read faster. No marking is drawn, which is fine as the text varies it.

## Figure 5.9  (fig-05-09.png, page p-152)

**Kind.** net fragment

**What the text says it models.** "The general scheme" for a net that weakly computes a polynomial Q. "The input values x_1, ..., x_n are encoded by x_i tokens in p_i", "Initially a token also resides in the 'run' place", and when done "the 'output' place will have y tokens, where y ≤ Q(x_1, ..., x_n)".

**Structure, briefly.** A rounded rectangle Q (the subnet, contents hidden). Interface places outside it: inputs p_1 ... p_n on the left, Output on the right, Run and Quit below. Arcs stop inside the box without reaching any visible node.

**Placement observations.**
1. The subnet is a rounded rectangle with only its name inside (top right). Arcs enter and stop with an arrowhead in empty interior space. *Says:* "this connects to something inside; you do not need to know what." *R: a black-box subnet is a rounded rectangle; arcs to hidden internals penetrate the border a short way and end with an arrowhead in empty space.*
2. Data inputs in a vertical column on the left, data output on the right, at roughly the height of the second input. *Says:* data flows left to right through Q, like a function box. *R: a computing subnet's data inputs line its left side, its result places sit on its right side.*
3. Control places (Run in, Quit out) go below the box, Run to the left of Quit. *Says:* control is a different axis from data. Start before end in reading order. *R: control places (start/stop) of a subnet sit on a side perpendicular to the data flow, entry before exit in reading order.*
4. The input column is a repeated element: p_1, p_2, vertical dots, p_n, evenly spaced, all the same size, with arcs entering at matching heights. *R: an indexed family of places is a column of identical nodes, with vertical ellipsis between the second and last.*
5. Place names here go outside to the left (p_1) while the symbolic marking (x_1) goes inside the circle. For Output, Run, Quit, the role name goes below. *R: symbolic marking inside the place; name outside.*
6. Interface places sit outside the box, not on its border. Arcs are straight; diagonal for Run and Quit.

**What could be better.** The Run and Quit arcs are diagonal for no visible reason; straight vertical would match the clean horizontal inputs. Output is not aligned with any input or with the box center, so its height looks arbitrary.

## Figure 5.10  (fig-05-10.png, page p-153)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "A multiplier subnet." t_1 moves one token from p_x to p_2; that enables t_3, which "can now copy y tokens from place p_y, putting them in p_3 and putting y tokens in p_x·y, the output place." Then t_2 returns the token to p_1, enabling t_4, "which can copy the y tokens from p_3 back into p_y. This entire process can be repeated exactly x times."

**Structure, briefly.** A control cycle p_1 → t_1 → p_2 → t_2 → p_1 with one token in p_1 (t_1 also consumes from p_x). Two copy loops gated by that control token: t_3 (self-loop on p_2) moves p_y to p_3 and p_x·y; t_4 (self-loop on p_1) moves p_3 back to p_y. Interface places p_x, p_y, and the output p_x·y.

**Placement observations.**
1. A dashed rounded rectangle marks the subnet boundary. p_x sits *on* the top edge (the dashed line runs into it from both sides) and p_y sits *on* the left edge. The output p_x·y sits outside, below, with its arc crossing the bottom edge. *Says:* inputs are the subnet's ports, shared with the enclosing net; the output is a place the subnet writes into but does not own (Fig. 5.12 merges outputs from many subnets). *R: a subnet's interface places sit on its boundary line, centered on it; a place the subnet only writes and that others share sits outside.* This is the clearest boundary statement in the chapter.
2. The control cycle is a horizontal pair: p_1 left (marked), p_2 right, t_2 on the straight line between them pointing left (return), t_1 above as a bypass arc going right. *Says:* the two control states and the two moves between them. Forward runs above, back runs on the straight line. *R: a two-state toggle is two places on one horizontal line; one transition on the direct segment, the other on an arc bowed to one side.*
3. The marked place p_1 is left, so the control cycle starts left, as text reads.
4. p_3 sits centered below the control line, between t_3 (right) and t_4 (left), with arcs that leave t_3 down, run horizontally into p_3 and out to t_4, and rise into it. *Says:* p_3 is the temporary between the two copy steps, and it mirrors the control cycle below it: t_3 under p_2, t_4 under p_1. *R: a buffer filled by one transition and drained by another sits on the line between them, below the controller whose states gate those transitions.*
5. t_3 sits directly below p_2 and t_4 directly below p_1, both horizontal bars. Each is gated by a short self-loop (two short vertical arcs, one down, one up). *Says:* "p_2 enables t_3" by stacking. *R: a transition gated by a place (self-loop) sits directly under that place, the loop drawn as two short parallel vertical arcs.*
6. Arcs are orthogonal with rounded corners: horizontal and vertical runs joined by quarter-circle bends. Different from the freehand curves of 5.6. *R: in a dense construction, route arcs orthogonally with rounded corners.*
7. p_y's arc to t_3 runs as a long horizontal along the bottom, under everything, then turns up into t_3. *Says:* p_y feeds the far side. It keeps out of the core.
8. Labels: below-right of places; transition labels below or right of bars. Output also has a role word "Output" under its name.

**What could be better.** The middle is dense: t_3 has five arcs on one short bar, two of them (p_y in, output out) passing within a few pixels of the self-loop arcs, and the p_x·y arc runs straight down through t_3's bar region. t_4's bar is crossed by the vertical arc from p_y to p_1, so it is hard to see which arcs touch t_4. The forward/backward reading of the control cycle is not obvious; t_1 is above and t_2 between, so the cycle runs clockwise only if you trace it.

## Figure 5.11  (fig-05-11.png, page p-155)

**Kind.** net fragment

**What the text says it models.** "A Petri net structure to weakly compute a term of a Diophantine polynomial. Each box is a net of the form of Figure 5.10." A term R_i = a_i · x_s1 · x_s2 · ... · x_sh is computed "by successive multiplication subnets".

**Structure, briefly.** A chain of h multiplier boxes. Each box shows only its two input places (one on its top edge, one on its left edge); its output arc feeds the next box's top place. The first box's top place holds the coefficient a_i. Each variable place x_sj feeds the left place of box j through one transition. The last box outputs to an Output place outside the dashed boundary.

**Placement observations.**
1. Each multiplier is a small solid square whose two input places sit on its border in the same positions as in 5.10: one centered on the top edge (p_x), one on the left edge (p_y). Interior empty except a dot. *Says:* "this is that subnet, collapsed"; the reader matches the port positions to 5.10. *R: a collapsed instance of a defined subnet keeps the same port positions as the subnet's own drawing.* Strong rule: port layout is the subnet's identity.
2. The boxes are stacked vertically, identical in size, evenly spaced, each output arc going straight down from the box's bottom edge to the next box's top place. *Says:* a pipeline of repeated stages; the running product flows down. *R: a chain of identical subnets is a straight column of identical boxes, each output feeding the next one's top port.*
3. The variable inputs x_s1, x_s2, ..., x_sh are a column on the left, each at the height of its box's left port, straight horizontal arcs through one bar. *Says:* stage j consumes variable j; same height means same stage. *R: per-stage side inputs line up across from their stage.*
4. The variable places sit *on* the dashed outer boundary (the dashes stop at each circle and resume below). Output sits outside, right, bottom. *R (as 5.10): ports on the boundary line, shared outputs outside.*
5. Elision of the middle stages is a vertical dotted run, both in the variable column and in the box chain, at the same height. *R: elide a repeated stage by dots at the same position in every parallel column.*
6. The formula R_i is written inside the dashed boundary at the bottom, as the boundary's label.
7. Coefficient a_i written inside the first box's top place, the symbolic marking convention again.

**What could be better.** The bars between x_sj and the box are unexplained in the figure, and since the box ports have no names, the reader must recall 5.10 to know which port is x and which is y. The output arc from the last box hooks right and crosses the dashed line in an otherwise empty area, fine.

## Figure 5.12  (fig-05-12.png, page p-156)

**Kind.** net fragment

**What the text says it models.** "A Petri net to weakly compute P(x_1, ..., x_n) by using a collection of subnets of the form in Figure 5.11." "The outputs of the k subnets for each term have been merged together, giving a total value which is the sum of each term."

**Structure, briefly.** k term boxes R_1 ... R_k, each with its variable places on its left edge. All three drawn outputs converge on one Output place. An outer solid rectangle labeled "P = R_1 + R_2 + ... + R_k".

**Placement observations.**
1. Term boxes stack vertically, identical size, evenly spaced, with vertical dots between R_2 and R_k (in both the box column and the port column). *R (as 5.11): repeated subnets form a column of identical boxes; elide with dots in every parallel column.*
2. Each box's variable places sit on its left edge, first and last only (x_s1 at top, x_sh at bottom), matching the left-side port column of 5.11 rotated into a box edge. *Says:* the term box of 5.11 collapsed, its port column kept. *R: a collapsed subnet keeps its ports on the same side as in its expanded drawing; a port family shows first and last only.*
3. The places also sit on the outer rectangle's left edge, so each is on two boundaries at once. *Says:* variables are inputs to the term and to the whole polynomial. *R: a port shared by nested subnets sits where their boundaries coincide.* Unclear whether coincidence of the two left edges is meant or just compact.
4. The outputs converge from each box's right edge to one Output place outside the outer rectangle, right, at the vertical center (level with R_2). The fan-in is symmetric: diagonal down from R_1, horizontal from R_2, diagonal up from R_k. *Says:* one place sums all terms. *R: a place merging k parallel subnets' outputs sits beyond them on the flow side, centered on the column, with arcs converging like a fan.*
5. Labels R_j at box centers; formula at the bottom inside the outer frame, as in 5.11.

**What could be better.** The outer rectangle's right edge is crossed by all three output arcs mid-length with no meaning attached; the Output place could be inside or the frame narrower. The vertical dots for the port column sit outside the outer frame, slightly misaligned with the box-column dots. Nothing marks the variables as shared across terms (x_1 appearing in R_1 and R_2 would be two separate circles here), which the text needs.

## Figure 5.13  (fig-05-13.png, page p-158)

**Kind.** net fragment

**What the text says it models.** Two constructed nets C_A' and C_B', to be compared. "In C_A', q and r are [isolated places,] q with one token." In C_B', r' is a "run" place: initially marked, every transition of C_B includes it as input and output. "A new transition transfers the enabling token from r' to q', disabling all transitions in C_B' and 'freezing' the marking." Then for each internal place p_i, one transition decrements it and one increments it, both using q' as a self-loop.

**Structure, briefly.** Two panels side by side. Each: the polynomial net (C_A or C_B, from 5.12) as a tall rectangle with p_1, p_2, ..., p_n on its left edge and an arc to Output; plus extra places. Left: r and q (q marked), isolated. Right: r' (marked) with a self-loop to representative t_j, a freeze transition r' → q', and q' with two self-loop transitions to one internal place p_i.

**Placement observations.**
1. The two nets are in separate outer frames, side by side, same size, with the shared parts (inner rectangle, port column, Output) at identical positions in each. *Says:* "these are the same except for the added parts"; the reader compares by looking at the same spot in each panel. *R: when two nets are to be compared, draw them side by side in matching frames with the common parts at identical positions; differences are then the only things that differ.*
2. The added places line up across panels: r and r' at the same height top right; q and q' as counterparts (q at second-row height left; q' lower right because it needs room for its gadget). Partly kept, partly broken. *R: corresponding added elements across compared panels share a position where the gadget allows.*
3. In C_B', the freeze chain r' → bar → q' is a straight vertical column on the right, bar horizontal. *Says:* run, then freeze, then adjust: control goes downward. 
4. The increment/decrement pair is two vertical bars stacked between p_i (left) and q' (right), forming a diamond with p_i and q'. Each bar has a self-loop pair of arcs to q' (one in, one out, slightly offset parallels) and one arc to or from p_i. *Says:* two symmetric opposite operations on p_i. *R: a pair of inverse transitions between the same two places stacks as mirror images on either side of the line joining the places.*
5. The internal place p_i sits outside the inner rectangle C_B, even though it is a place of C_B. Only the port places sit on the rectangle edge. t_j likewise sits outside the inner rectangle. Unclear whether this is deliberate (show "a representative") or for room. It contradicts the inside/outside rule of 5.2 and 5.6.
6. Panel names C_A', C_B' are set below the frames, centered; inner names C_A, C_B inside the inner rectangle near the bottom.
7. Port places sit stacked on the inner rectangle's left edge, the 5.12 convention.

**What could be better.** In C_A', r and q float unconnected, which is correct, but they sit where the reader expects a gadget. The r'/t_j arcs run diagonally across the inner rectangle edge with no visual cue. Placing p_i and t_j outside C_B misstates what belongs to which net; a reader following 5.2 would read them as added elements.

## Figure 5.14  (fig-05-14.png, page p-159)

**Kind.** net fragment

**What the text says it models.** Nets A and B share places p_1 ... p_n. Run places r_A, r_B; every transition of A uses r_A as input and output, likewise for B. A marked place s and transitions t_A, t_B: t_A takes s and "produces the initial marking for net A plus a token in r_A", t_B likewise for B. So C behaves as A or as B, chosen once. "The net D is obtained from C by adding one new transition, q_B" (input r_B, no output); "E is constructed from D by adding a new transition, q_A".

**Structure, briefly.** A top-level choice (s to t_A or t_B), each branch initializing the shared places and its own run place. Two run places with self-loops to their subnet's transitions. Nested frames: A and B overlap in the shared column of p's; C contains both; D adds q_B; E adds q_A.

**Placement observations.**
1. Three concentric rectangles, innermost C, then D, then E. Each larger frame adds exactly one transition, placed in the band between it and the frame inside: q_B in D's band (top right), q_A in E's band (top left). Frame labels D and E sit in their band next to their added transition. *Says:* "E ⊃ D ⊃ C", each extension by one element. *R: a chain of incremental extensions is nested frames; each frame's addition sits in the strip that frame adds, next to the frame's name.* Strong and economical.
2. A and B are overlapping rectangles. The overlap is a column holding p_1, ..., p_n. A's private transitions sit in A's left part, B's in B's right part. *Says:* the shared places are exactly what the two subnets have in common, literally the intersection of two regions (a Venn layout). *R: places shared by two subnets sit in the overlap of their regions; each subnet's private elements sit in its own non-overlapping part.*
3. The figure is left-right mirror symmetric about the vertical through the p column and s: A, r_A, t_A, q_A, E on the left; B, r_B, t_B, q_B, D on the right. *Says:* A and B are interchangeable roles. *R: two roles symmetric in the construction are mirror images about a vertical axis; shared elements sit on the axis.* The symmetry breaks only in frame nesting (D right, E left), which the text explains.
4. The initial choice sits at the bottom: s (marked) centered on the axis, t_A and t_B up-left and up-right, arcs curving out like a Y. Their outputs rise to the p column (crossing in a narrow X) and to their run places on the outside. *Says:* start at the bottom, flow upward; this net reads bottom to top. Unusual for Peterson. Unclear why; it may be just to keep q_A and q_B near the top frames.
5. Each run place sits outside its subnet rectangle, level with the middle of the subnet, with self-loop pairs (curved lens pairs) to two representative transitions shown as short horizontal bars inside the subnet. Vertical dots between the two bars elide the rest. *R: a run place that every transition of a subnet self-loops with sits outside the subnet on the side away from the shared column, its loops drawn to two representative transitions with dots between.*
6. q_A and q_B are horizontal bars at the top, above their run places, fed by long straight vertical arcs. *Says:* a drain hung off the run place. 
7. Labels: frame names large italic at a corner of each frame; place labels above-left or above-right, outward.

**What could be better.** The outputs of t_A and t_B to p_1 and p_n cross each other inside the shared column and run close together, so it is hard to see that both t_A and t_B write all the p's. The bottom-to-top flow is the opposite of the q-drains, which also point up, so "up" means both "initialize" and "drain"; consistent direction but no starting cue other than the token.

## Figure 5.15  (fig-05-15.png, page p-163)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Testing for zero with a complementary pair: "two places p and p' such that μ(p) + μ(p') is a constant". "For small numbers k one can test if the marking of a place is k by having the place be an input to a transition k times." The caption: testing a bounded place "for a marking of 0, 1 or 2. **Structure, briefly.** C_1 and C_2 have the identical 6.10-C_2 shape (spine c, c, c; a-loop and b-loop with a counter above), differing only in arc weights to the counter (3 and 2 vs 2 and 3). C': p_s' → c → a pair of places (one per operand, upper and lower) → merged a (self-loop on both, writes both counters) → merged c → pair → merged b → pair → c → p_f'. The two counters sit at top and bottom center.

**Placement observations.**
1. The operands are drawn identically (same positions for every node), so the eye diffs them and finds only the weights changed. *R (as 5.13): nets meant for comparison share an identical layout.*
2. In C', each operand place becomes one of a vertical pair: C_1's place above the axis, C_2's corresponding place below, at the same x. Each merged transition sits on the axis between the pair. The result is a single left-to-right axis p_s' to p_f' with paired places mirrored above and below it. *Says:* lockstep: the two nets advance together, stage by stage; the axis is the shared word, the upper and lower halves are each operand's state. *R: in a synchronous product, the shared transitions lie on the central axis; the two operands' places mirror each other above and below it, corresponding places at the same x.*
3. The merged loop transitions (a, b) are horizontal bars on the axis between their two places, with self-loop pairs going up to the upper place and down to the lower place. *Says:* one transition touching both halves equally.
4. The two counters move to the top center (C_1's) and bottom center (C_2's), and their weighted arcs become wide elliptical arcs around the middle: the whole C' looks like an eye or an orbit. *Says:* each counter belongs to its operand's half. *R: an operand's coupling place stays on that operand's side of the axis, at the outer edge.*
5. The figure is close to symmetric top-bottom and left-right (a mirrored by b, c by c).
6. Start and final are on the axis at its ends; the first and last c's fork and join to the place pairs with straight diagonals.
7. Labels: transition letters above the bar or to the side of the free end; inner places unnamed.

**What could be better.** The strong symmetry makes the structure obvious, but the weighted arcs (2 and 3 parallel curves) to the counters sweep across the central c transition's arcs, and the crossing diagonals around the central c (upper place to lower-right place and so on) read as an X whose meaning is not obvious: the central c takes from both left places and gives to both right places. Neither operand is recognizable in C' as its own drawing; the redraw keeps correspondence (same x) but not the operand's shape. That is arguably right for intersection, where the operands lose independence.

## Figure 6.12  (fig-06-12.png, page p-194)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "A Petri net which generates the context-free language {a^n b^n | n > 1}. Since this language is not regular, we know that not all Petri net languages are regular languages."

**Structure, briefly.** Spine p_s → a → place → b → place → b → p_f. An a self-loop above the first inner place and a b self-loop above the second. A counter place above both loops, written by the loop a and read by the loop b and by the final b.

**Placement observations.**
1. Start far left, final far right, one straight horizontal spine, evenly spaced, spine bars vertical with letters below. *R (as 6.8).*
2. Loops above their places as horizontal bars with two short vertical arcs; letters at the outer bar end (a at left, b at left). *R (as 6.2 and 6.8): repeat loops above the spine, directly over their place.*
3. The counter sits above the loops, between them, slightly right of the a loop. Its second output (to the final b on the spine) runs as a long arc swinging right and down to the spine bar, passing outside the b loop. *Says:* the counter gates the end of the word too. *R: an arc from the coupling layer down to the spine goes around the outside of intervening loops, not through them.*
4. This is the 6.2 net in standard form, and the same picture as C_2 in 6.8. Peterson reuses one layout for one language across figures. *R: draw the same net the same way everywhere it appears.*

**What could be better.** The counter is pulled left of center, so its arc to the final b has to make a long sweep; centered over the b loop the arc would be shorter, but then the a arc would lengthen. A fine drawing; little to fault.

## Figure 6.13  (fig-06-13.png, page p-195)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "A Petri net language which is context-sensitive but not context-free": {a^n b^n c^n | n > 0}.

**Structure, briefly.** Spine p_s → a → place → b → place → c → p_f. Loops above each of the three right-hand spine places: a, b, c. Two counters above: the first written by the a-loop and read by the b-loop; the second written by the b-loop and read by the c-loop. The c loop sits on p_f itself.

**Placement observations.**
1. Same spine, same loop-above idiom as 6.12, extended by one phase. *R (as 6.8, 6.12).*
2. The counters form a second row above the loops, each centered between the loop that writes it and the loop that reads it, as an arch. Two arches side by side, sharing the b loop as their common foot. *Says:* a chain of handoffs, a-count to b, b-count to c, read left to right like the spine below. *R: a coupling place between consecutive loops sits in an arch above them, centered between writer and reader; a chain of such couplings is a row of arches parallel to the spine.*
3. The b loop bar is hit by four arcs (two loop arcs, one in from the first arch, one out to the second); the layout puts the incoming arch on the left half of the bar and the outgoing on the right half. *Says:* left half is "from before", right half "to after". *R: arcs on a bar are ordered along it to match the flow direction: from-left arrivals on the left, rightward departures on the right.*
4. Labels below spine bars, at the left end of loop bars. Inner places unnamed; p_s, p_f named inside.
5. Even spacing on the spine; the arches are identical in size and shape. *R: repeated structural units get identical geometry.*

**What could be better.** The c loop sits on p_f, so the final place has arcs back out, which the standard form forbids; the drawing gives no visual hint of this. Otherwise very clean: this and 6.12 are the model for the language nets.

## Figure 6.14  (fig-06-14.png, page p-199)

**Kind.** net (three operand nets and their combination)

**What the text says it models.** {x^i W y^i | i ≥ 0} from standard-form nets for x, y, and W, plus "a new place p so that each time γ_x is executed, a token is placed in p. The place p counts the number of times γ_x is executed. After x has executed as many times as it wishes, γ_W is executed, and finally γ_y is executed repeatedly, removing one token from p for each repetition." Example x = ab, y = b(a+b), W = b^+ a.

**Structure, briefly.** Three small operand nets stacked at top, each a horizontal spine p_s → ... → p_f: x (a, b), W (b, then a b-loop above the inner place, then a), y (b, then an a-or-b diamond). The combination: W's spine horizontal in the middle; x drawn as a vertical column on the left (p_sx top, inner place, p_fx bottom); y as a vertical column on the right (p_sy top, inner place, a/b pair, p_fy bottom). Repetition cycles of x close through p_sw on the left; repetitions of y close through p_fw on the right. Counter p at top center, written by the x-cycle's a transition and read by the y-cycle's b transition.

**Placement observations.**
1. The operands are stacked as in 6.8 to 6.11, each with p_s left and p_f right, but without formulas under them.
2. In the combination, W keeps its horizontal orientation and becomes the central axis. x and y are *rotated* 90 degrees into vertical columns, x at the left end of W and y at the right end. *Says:* W is the middle of the word; x^i before it and y^i after it; the rotation turns the repeated parts into side towers so that their repetition cycles can hook into W's start and end. *R: in a composite whose middle part runs once and whose outer parts repeat, lay the once-part on the horizontal axis and the repeated parts as vertical columns at its ends.* The reading order left to right is still x, W, y.
3. The x and y columns run top to bottom, p_s at top, p_f at bottom; their start and final places are kept even though the construction's flow passes sideways through W. The original column order is visible.
4. The repetition cycles are small diamonds between each column's inner place and W's start or end place: on the left, inner-x to b to p_sw to a back to inner-x; on the right, the mirror between p_fw and inner-y. *Says:* the "go round again" path, local to the junction. *R: a repetition cycle joining a column to the axis is a small diamond between them, its two transitions above and below the line joining the two places.*
5. Counter p at top center, above W, at the midpoint between the x-cycle's writer and the y-cycle's reader, with two straight diagonals. *Says:* p links the x side and the y side across W. *R (as 6.2, 6.13): a place coupling two loops sits above the axis, centered between writer and reader.*
6. W's b-loop has moved from above (in the operand) to below the spine in the combination, freeing the space above for p's arcs. *Says:* loop side is chosen to stay clear of the coupling layer. *R (refines 6.8): a loop goes on the side of the spine opposite the coupling layer.*
7. Left-right mirror symmetry overall: x column and y column, the two diamonds, p centered.
8. Labels: letters beside each bar's free end; place names inside.

**What could be better.** The mirror symmetry suggests x and y are symmetric roles, which is roughly true, but y's internal structure (a/b pair at the bottom, under the inner place) differs from x's single b, so the columns are not equal heights. The diamonds put four bars close together around p_sw and p_fw, and the bar labels (a, b) crowd. Rotating x and y loses the at-a-glance match with the operand drawings above; a reader must recognize the operands rotated.
