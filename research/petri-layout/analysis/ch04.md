# Chapter 4 figures: placement analysis

## Figure 4.1  (fig-04-01.png, page p-097)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Nothing physical. It is "a simple Petri net" that is not safe, used as the input to the forced-safeness construction of Figure 4.2. Safeness is defined as no place ever holding more than one token.

**Structure, briefly.** A straight chain t1 -> p1 -> t2 -> p2 -> t3. t1 is a source transition (no inputs), t3 a sink. p3 holds one token and forms a self-loop with t2 (input and output). Unsafe because t1 can fire repeatedly and pile tokens into p1.

**Placement observations.**
1. The chain lies on one horizontal line: t1 at the far left, then p1, t2, p2, t3 at the far right, evenly spaced. *Says:* the net is one pipeline, read left to right, with a source at the left end and a sink at the right end. *R: a chain whose ends are a source and a sink is one straight line, source first in reading direction, sink last.*
2. The side place p3 sits directly above t2, off the main line, and is the only marked place. *Says:* p3 is not part of the pipeline; it is a condition t2 consults and restores. Putting it above, perpendicular to the flow, makes "off to the side" literal. *R: a place that only forms a self-loop with one transition sits perpendicular to the main line, directly opposite that transition.*
3. The self-loop is a narrow lens of two curved arcs between p3 and t2, one down, one up, bowing to opposite sides. *Says:* "taken and given back" in one gesture; the symmetry makes the pair read as one relation. *R: a self-loop pair is drawn as two mirror-image curves between the place and the transition, one each way.*
4. Bars are vertical, across the horizontal flow. t1 and t3 are long full-height bars; t2 is a very short tick, almost a point, where four arcs meet. Unclear whether the short t2 is deliberate or an artifact of crowding the arc heads; it does make t2 hard to see as a transition. *R: a bar is perpendicular to the flow; keep it long enough to read as a bar even when many arcs land on it.*
5. All labels sit below their element on the main line (t1, p1, t2, p2, t3 in one row under the line). p3's label is to its upper right. *Says:* the label row repeats the reading order. *R: labels of a horizontal chain go in one row on the same side, the side no arc uses.*
6. Main-line arcs are straight; only the self-loop curves. *R: straight along the flow, curved only off it.*

**What could be better.** t2 is nearly invisible as a bar; the reader infers it from the label. The unsafe place (p1) is not marked out in any way, though the text does not need it to be.

## Figure 4.2  (fig-04-02.png, page p-097)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Figure 4.1 "forced to be safe". Each place p_i to be made safe gets a complement place p_i' that holds a token exactly when p_i does not; transitions that consume from p_i deposit into p_i' and vice versa.

**Structure, briefly.** Figure 4.1 unchanged, plus p1' and p2', each marked. p1' is output of t2 and input of t1; p2' is output of t3 and input of t2. So t1 -> p1 -> t2 -> p1' -> t1 and t2 -> p2 -> t3 -> p2' -> t2 become two small cycles sharing t2. p3 is untouched.

**Placement observations.**
1. The original net keeps its exact arrangement: the same horizontal line t1, p1, t2, p2, t3, with p3 above t2 and the same self-loop lens. *Says:* this is Figure 4.1 with something added; the reader compares the two at a glance and sees only the new elements as new. *R: when a figure is a transformation of an earlier one, keep every original node in its original relative position and add the new nodes in previously empty space.*
2. The new complement places sit below the line, each directly under the gap of the place it complements (p1' under p1, p2' under p2). *Says:* p_i' pairs with p_i; vertical alignment states "this is p_i's partner". Below the line is the side that was empty in 4.1 (opposite p3). *R: a complement place sits directly opposite its partner across the main line, on the side free of other additions.*
3. Each complement arc pair forms a shallow arch under the line: t2 -> p1' -> t1 curves back leftward, t3 -> p2' -> t2 curves back leftward. With the straight forward arcs above, each p_i, p_i' pair reads as a small loop: forward along the top, back along the bottom. *Says:* "the token returns" as a backward flow below the forward one. *R: a return path runs parallel to the forward path, on the opposite side from other attachments, curving back against the flow.*
4. The whole is mirror-like left and right of t2: two identical returns. *Says:* the same construction applied twice. *R: identical constructions applied to two places are drawn as mirror or translated copies.*
5. Labels moved: in 4.1 t1, p1, t2, p2, t3 were labeled below; now the line labels are above (t1, p1, p2, t3) and t2 is below, because the bottom is now taken by arcs and complement places. The new places are labeled below. *Says:* labels yield to arcs. *R: labels go on whichever side is free after arcs and nodes are placed, even if that breaks consistency with a previous figure.*
6. The complements are marked (one token each, placed centered). The token placement again is center of place. *Says:* initial state "p_i empty so p_i' full" is visible.

**What could be better.** t2 is still a near-invisible point, now with six arcs meeting there; the left arc into t2 from p2' and the arc out to p1' meet at the same tick, hard to read which is which. Label sides flipped relative to 4.1, slightly weakening the "same net" comparison.

## Figure 4.3  (fig-04-03.png, page p-099)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Introduced under conservation, which the text motivates by resource allocation ("a set of three line printers is represented by a place with an initial marking of three tokens"). The net is "not strictly conservative since the firing of either transition t1 or t3 will decrease the number of tokens by one, while firing transition t2 or t4 will add a token" (OCR; the parity of the transition names in that sentence looks garbled, but the point is that token count changes). It is conservative with weights (1, 1, 2, 2, 1). The shape is the classic two-process mutual exclusion net, though the text does not name it so here.

**Structure, briefly.** Two cycles: p1 -> t1 -> p3 -> t3 -> p1 and p2 -> t2 -> p4 -> t4 -> p2. p1, p2 marked. Shared place p5, marked, is an input of t1 and t2 and an output of t3 and t4. So t1, t2 are synchronizations (two inputs, one output) and t3, t4 forks (one input, two outputs).

**Placement observations.**
1. Each process is a vertical column: marked place at top, bar, place, bar, top to bottom. Left column p1, t1, p3, t3; right column p2, t2, p4, t4. *Says:* each process is one sequence read downward, starting at its token. *R: a process cycle is cut open at its marked place and laid as a straight column, marked place first.*
2. The two columns are mirror images about the vertical center line, with matching nodes at equal heights. *Says:* the two processes are identical peers; same height means same stage. *R: identical processes are drawn as mirror images, stages aligned across.*
3. The shared place p5 sits on the center line, between the columns, at a height between the acquire bars (t1, t2) and the release bars (t3, t4), closer to the acquire bars. *Says:* p5 belongs to neither process; it is held during the stretch between acquire and release. *R: a resource shared by two processes sits on their axis of symmetry, vertically between the transitions that take it and those that return it.*
4. The acquire arcs leave the top of p5 and arch up and outward into the inner end of t1 and t2. The release arcs leave the inner ends of t3, t4 by a small downward hook, then sweep up and inward into the bottom of p5. The four arcs form an X-like butterfly centered on p5. *Says:* p5 is lent out at the top and comes back from the bottom; the inner side of each column is "the resource side". *R: arcs to and from a shared place attach on the inner end of each bar, the end facing the shared place.*
5. Each cycle's return arc (t3 -> p1, t4 -> p2) leaves the outer end of the bottom bar with a downward hook, swings out wide along the outer edge, and enters the top place from above-outside. *Says:* "and repeat", on the side away from the shared resource. *R: a cycle's return arc runs on the outer side of its column, away from what the process shares, and enters the first place from above.*
6. Bars are horizontal, across a vertical flow. Each bar is drawn longer to the inner side, giving room for the second arc. Labels sit on the outer end (t1, t3 left; t2, t4 right). Place labels are on the outer side too (p1, p3 left; p2, p4 right), p5 above. *R: transition and place labels go on the outer side of a column, the side facing away from the shared center.*
7. Outputs leaving a bar downward to two destinations exit as two separate stubs: the hooks at t3 and t4 descend from the bar a short way before curving. *Says:* each output is a distinct arc, visibly parted at the bar. *R: when a bar has two outputs, they leave from separate points along the bar, ordered by the side their target is on.*
8. Tokens are single centered dots; all three marked places are at the top half of the figure. *Says:* the initial state is "everyone at the start, resource free".

**What could be better.** The return arcs swing very wide and very tall; they dominate the outline of the figure more than the processes do. The weights (1, 1, 2, 2, 1) the text uses are invisible; the drawing gives no hint why p3 and p4 weigh 2.

## Figure 4.4  (fig-04-04.png, page p-099)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "A strictly conservative Petri net which is equivalent to the net of Figure 4.3." Every transition now has as many outputs as inputs.

**Structure, briefly.** Figure 4.3 with each arc t1 -> p3, p3 -> t3, t2 -> p4, p4 -> t4 doubled (multiplicity 2). Nothing else changes.

**Placement observations.**
1. Every node of 4.3 is in the same place: same columns, same p5 position, same return arcs, same butterfly, same labels. *Says:* the reader compares 4.3 and 4.4 and sees the only change. *R: a transformed net keeps every node position and arc route of its original; only the change is new.*
2. Multiplicity 2 is drawn as two parallel straight arcs side by side, not as a number on one arc. The second arc of each pair sits on the inner side (toward p5), and the bars are extended inward to receive it. *Says:* p3 now carries two tokens' worth per pass; the doubled line makes the "weight 2" of p3, p4 visible, which is exactly why 4.3 was conservative with weights (1, 1, 2, 2, 1). Placement turns the hidden weighting into visible thickness. *R: a small multiplicity is drawn as that many parallel arcs, offset toward the free side, with the bars widened to take them.*
3. Because the arcs are parallel and straight, each column now looks like a doubled rail from t1 down to t3. *Says:* the critical section stretch is "heavier", which fits the conservation story: the resource token is inside it. Inference; the text does not say this.
4. The release hooks from t3, t4 into p5 and the outer returns stay attached at the same ends of the bars, so the bars' extra length goes into the middle of the bar. *R: when an element grows to take more arcs, existing attachments keep their side and the new ones go between.*

**What could be better.** Doubling by parallel lines is fine at 2 but would not scale; the book elsewhere uses numbers or multiple arcs inconsistently. The p5 butterfly arcs now nearly touch the inner rails near t1 and t2.

## Figure 4.5  (fig-04-05.png, page p-101)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Only "a nonconservative Petri net", given as the counterexample after 4.3 is shown conservative under weights (1, 1, 2, 2, 1). No modeled system.

**Structure, briefly.** No labels. Two cycles of two places and two transitions, each with a marked top place. A middle place is written by the left cycle's bottom transition (which also returns to its own top place, so one input, two outputs) and read by the right cycle's top transition (two inputs, one output). It is the producer/consumer shape of Figure 3.29 with the buffer placed diagonally.

**Placement observations.**
1. Same frame as 4.3: two vertical columns, marked place at top, horizontal bars, outer return loops. *Says:* the reader sees the 4.3 family and attends to what differs. *R: a net presented as a variant of a previous one reuses its frame.*
2. The middle place sits between the columns at the height of the columns' lower places, and its two arcs form one diagonal stroke: from the left bottom bar up-right into the place, then on up-right into the right top bar. *Says:* tokens flow one way, left to right, from the left process's end to the right process's start. A single rising diagonal reads as a conveyor, not a loop; there is no return arc to the left, and that absence is what nonconservation is about here. *R: a place that couples a writer to a reader sits between them on the line from writer to reader, so its in and out arcs form one continuous stroke.*
3. Compared to p5 in 4.3, which had arcs back and forth on both sides, this place has one in and one out. Its asymmetric position (lower than p5 was, and between bottom-left and top-right) is the only hint that it is a one-way buffer, not a shared resource. Inference.
4. The left bottom bar's two outputs leave by hooks: outer hook to the return, inner hook to the buffer. Same idiom as 4.3. *R: two outputs of one bar leave from opposite ends, return on the outside, coupling on the inside.*
5. No labels at all. The figure is about the shape, not names, and the text refers to no element. *R: omit labels when the text names no element.*

**What could be better.** With no labels and the 4.3 frame, a reader has to count arcs to see why it is nonconservative; nothing in the layout marks the transition that creates a token. The left and right columns are no longer symmetric in role but are still drawn as mirrors, which slightly suggests a symmetry the net lacks.

## Figure 4.6  (fig-04-06.png, page p-102)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "A system with two different resources q and r and two processes a and b." Process a requests q then r, then releases both; process b requests r then q, then releases both. q is p4, r is p5. "Consider the sequence which starts t1 t4: Process a has q and wants r; process b has r and wants q. The system is deadlocked."

**Structure, briefly.** Process a: p1 (marked) -> t1 -> p2 -> t2 -> p3 -> t3 -> p1. Process b: p6 (marked) -> t4 -> p7 -> t5 -> p8 -> t6 -> p6. p4 (q, marked) feeds t1 and t5; p5 (r, marked) feeds t2 and t4. t3 and t6 each return both p4 and p5.

**Placement observations.**
1. Two vertical columns, each a process, headed by a text label "Process a" / "Process b" above the column. Marked start place at top, then bar, place, bar, place, bar. Stages aligned across: t1 level with t4, p2 with p7, t2 with t5, p3 with p8, t3 with t6. *Says:* two peer processes with the same number of steps; same height means same step number, so "first request", "second request", "release" are rows. *R: parallel processes are columns with corresponding steps on shared rows; a column may carry a process-name heading above it.*
2. The two resources sit in the center gap, one above the other: p4 (q) near the first-request row (just below t1/t4), p5 (r) at the second-request row (level with t2/t5). *Says:* the resources are shared, so they belong to the middle; their vertical order echoes the order process a acquires them. *R: shared resources sit on the axis between the processes, ordered vertically by when the left (first-drawn) process acquires them.*
3. Because process b acquires them in the opposite order, its acquire arcs cross: p4 -> t5 runs from upper center down-right, and p5 -> t4 runs from lower center up-right, and they cross in an X between p4/p5 and process b. Process a's acquire arcs, in contrast, are two short parallel arcs going left at their own heights. *Says:* the crossing is the deadlock. The reader sees at once that b grabs the resources "crosswise" to a, which is exactly the opposite-order acquisition that permits deadlock. Placement makes the ordering conflict a visible X. This is a case where a crossing carries meaning and should not be removed. *R: when the structure is a conflict of order, keep both sides' order on the same axis so that the inversion appears as a crossing; do not re-place the shared nodes to untangle it.*
4. The releases all come from the bottom bars t3 and t6: each emits a fan of hooks that sweep up into the bottoms of p4 and p5, plus the outer return to the process's top place. The left bottom bar's arcs go to p4 (long) and p5 (shorter); the right's mirror them. *Says:* "release both at the end", visibly from one bar. *R: multiple outputs of one bar leave from points along the bar ordered by where their targets lie; releases enter the shared place from below.*
5. Return arcs are wide outer loops, leaving the bottom bar downward and entering the top place from above-outside, as in 4.3. *R: as in 4.3, returns run outside, away from the shared center.*
6. Acquire arcs enter the inner end of each bar, from the side toward the center. Bar labels sit on the outer end (t1, t2, t3 left; t4, t5, t6 right), place labels on the outer side, resource labels above the resource. *R: labels on the outer side; the inner side belongs to coupling arcs.*
7. Spacing is uniform down each column; the columns are wide apart to leave room for two stacked resources and their arcs.

**What could be better.** The bottom of the figure is crowded: four release arcs leave t3 and t6 and cross the acquire arcs on their way up, so the release structure is harder to read than the acquire structure. The meaningful X (b's crossed acquires) shares the middle with two unrelated crossings from the release arcs, which dilutes the message. Placing p4 and p5 so the release arcs come up the outsides of the center area could keep the X as the only crossing; not certain that is possible without other costs.

## Figure 4.7  (fig-04-07.png, page p-103)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Levels of liveness. "Transition t0 can never fire; it is dead. Transition t1 can fire exactly once; it is live at level 1." t2 can fire as many times as t3 fired before t1 (level 2). t3 can fire infinitely often until t1 fires (level 3).

**Structure, briefly.** Four places, unlabeled: left L (marked), right R, top T, bottom B. t1 moves the token L -> R. t3 has a self-loop with L and outputs to T (a counter). t2 has a self-loop with R and consumes from T. t0 needs both L and R and outputs to B. Since the single token is in L or R, never both, t0 is dead.

**Placement observations.**
1. The one-shot move L -> t1 -> R is a horizontal line across the middle, t1 as a vertical bar at the center. *Says:* the net's one irreversible step is its spine: before t1 is "left", after t1 is "right". *R: a single irreversible step that splits the net's life into before and after is the central horizontal axis, before on the left.*
2. t3 sits directly above L, t2 directly above R, each joined to its place by two short parallel vertical arcs (one up, one down). *Says:* each is a self-loop that leaves its place's token in place; the tight parallel pair reads "test, don't consume". *R: a transition in a self-loop with a place sits right next to it, perpendicular to the main axis, joined by two short parallel arcs.*
3. T sits top center, between t3 and t2, joined by an orthogonal-ish arc over the top: up from t3, right into T, right out of T, down into t2. *Says:* T is a channel from the "before" side to the "after" side, carried over the top in parallel to t1. Counting with T happens on the upper level; the lower level is the dead join. *R: a place that carries tokens from one phase's transition to the next phase's sits midway between them on a route parallel to the main axis.*
4. t0 sits bottom center, below t1, with diagonal arcs from L and R converging into it, and B below it. *Says:* t0 needs both sides at once. Its position under the center, equidistant from L and R, makes the join symmetric and shows it needs both of two things that the spine above says are never true together (the token is either left or right of t1). The deadness is readable from geometry: the join reaches back to both ends of a one-way trip. *R: a synchronizing transition sits on the perpendicular bisector of its inputs, on the side away from other structure, its output continuing the same direction.*
5. Bar orientation: t1 vertical across a horizontal flow; t3, t2, t0 horizontal across vertical arcs. *R: bar perpendicular to its dominant arc direction.*
6. Only transitions are labeled; labels sit to the outer side (t3 left, t2 right, t0 right) or below (t1). Places have no labels because the text never names them. Token centered in L.
7. Overall the figure is left-right symmetric about the vertical through T, t1, t0, B. The symmetry mirrors the structure: t3 and t2 play mirror roles (producer and consumer of T).

**What could be better.** The level-of-liveness story runs left to right in time (t3 many times, then t1, then t2), and the layout supports it, but nothing distinguishes t0 as dead; a reader must reason it out. Places have no labels, so the text's argument can only refer to transitions; that works here but would not if places were discussed.

## Figure 4.8  (fig-04-08.png, page p-105)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Hyman's 1966 "solution" to mutual exclusion for two processes, which "purports to solve the mutual exclusion problem: places p4 and p9 are expected to be mutually exclusive." The question is whether any state is reachable with p4 >= 1 and p9 >= 1, or equivalently whether a sequence like t3 sigma t9 can occur. The caption: "Due to the constant use of places k0, k1, b1, and b0, these are repeated in the graph to make it easier to read. All places labeled k0 are the same place." k0/k1 encode the turn variable k; b0/b1 encode each process's flag.

**Structure, briefly.** p1 -> t1, an initializing fork that outputs b0, k0, b1 (the shared variables' initial values) and p2, p7 (the two processes' starts). Process 0 (left): p2 -> t2 (consumes b0, i.e. sets its flag) -> p3. p3 is a choice. Inner branch: t3 (tests k0 by self-loop) -> p4 (critical section) -> t4 -> outputs b0 (clears flag) and returns to p2. Outer branch: t5 (tests k1) -> p5 -> t6 (tests b1) -> p6 -> t7 (consumes k1, produces k0) -> back to p3. Process 1 (right) is the mirror: p7 -> t8 (consumes b1) -> p8; inner branch t9 (tests k1) -> p9 (critical) -> t10 -> b1, back to p7; outer branch t11 (tests k0) -> p10 -> t12 (tests b0) -> p11 -> t13 (consumes k0, produces k1) -> back to p8. No token dots are drawn anywhere, not even in p1. I take p1 to be the marked start, but the figure does not show it.

**Placement observations.**
1. The whole net is a top-down tree: p1 at the apex on the center line, t1 just under it, then a wide inverted V of two long diagonal arcs down to p2 (left) and p7 (right). *Says:* one start, then two processes that run from then on. The reader starts at the top point and reads down. *R: an initializing transition that spawns several processes sits at the top center, and the processes hang below it, spread symmetrically.*
2. The shared variables' initial values are emitted by t1 as three short arcs to b0, k0, b1, set in a tight row just under t1 between the two long diagonals, and the long diagonals are the outer two of the five outputs. *Says:* "initialize the globals, then start both processes". The globals are drawn small and close to their creator; the processes are drawn far. Initial marking is expressed by structure (a fork from p1) rather than by token dots. *R: outputs of one transition fan out in a row ordered by where they lead: short ones to nearby leaves in the middle, long ones to distant subtrees at the ends.*
3. Each process is a column: p2, t2, p3 straight down (p7, t8, p8 on the right). At p3 the column splits into two sub-columns, one directly below p3 on each side, reached by two curved arcs that bow outward symmetrically like a wishbone. *Says:* p3 is a decision; both branches start at the same row (t5 level with t3), so they read as alternatives, not a sequence. *R: a choice place's alternatives start on the same row, spread symmetrically below it.*
4. Which branch goes where is consistent across the two processes and meaningful. For each process the critical-section branch is the inner one (p4 right of the left process, p9 left of the right process), and the retry loop is the outer one. So the two critical sections p4 and p9 sit closest to each other, at the same height, facing across the center gap. *Says:* the two places that are "expected to be mutually exclusive" are visually paired. This is the property the figure is about, and the layout places its two subject places side by side where a reader compares them. *R: when a figure exists to ask whether two places can be marked together, place those two at the same height, adjacent across the axis of symmetry.*
5. The two processes are mirror images about the vertical center, row for row: p2/p7, t2/t8, p3/p8, then t5|t3 mirrored to t9|t11, and so on down to t7/t13 and their outputs k0/k1 at the bottom. *Says:* the two processes run the same code with indices swapped. The mirror makes Hyman's symmetry claim visible. *R: two processes that run the same algorithm with swapped roles are drawn as exact mirror images; corresponding transitions sit on the same row.*
6. Shared variables are replicated. Every arc to b0, b1, k0, k1 goes to a local copy placed right beside the bar, on the outer side of that sub-column. A test (self-loop) is drawn as a C-shaped pass-through: an arc from a copy above the bar's end curves into the bar, and an arc from the bar curves out to a second copy below (t5, t6, t3, t9, t11, t12). A consume is one copy above with one arc in (t2, t8 from upper side; t7, t13 also take k1/k0 from a copy at their outer side), a produce is one copy below with one arc out (t4 -> b0, t7 -> k0, t10 -> b1, t13 -> k1). *Says:* a global is a condition the step reads locally; the reader sees what each step needs without chasing long arcs. The C-shape reads "passes through unchanged" because the token visibly comes in and goes out. *R: a heavily shared place may be drawn as local copies, one beside each transition that uses it, input copy before the bar in reading order and output copy after; say in the caption that copies are one place.*
7. Copies sit on the outer side of each sub-column, so the space between the left process's two sub-columns and the center gap stay free for return arcs. The returns: t7 -> p3 runs straight up between the two sub-columns into the bottom of p3 (retry the decision); t4 -> p2 leaves t4's inner end with a hook and swings in a tall arc on the inner side, up to p2's lower-right. Mirror on the right (t13 -> p8, t10 -> p7). *Says:* "retry" goes back to the choice, "done with critical section" goes back to the very start, and both are long upward arcs, distinguishable by height. The returns use the inner side because the outer side holds the variable copies. *R: a return arc runs on whichever side of its column is free of coupling elements; how far up it reaches shows how far back the process goes.*
8. Bars are horizontal across vertical flow. Bars carrying a variable test are longer, extended toward the copy side. Transition labels mostly sit at the bar's end on the copy side, tucked inside the C curve (t5, t6, t3, t11, t12), but t2 and t8 have the label on the side opposite their copy, and t7, t13 on the side with copies. So label side is not one rule; it goes where there is room. Unclear if deliberate.
9. Place labels are inside small circles, unlike most other figures in this chapter where labels sit beside larger circles. *Says:* in a dense net, the place itself carries its name, and the duplicated globals need their name inside to be identifiable at all. *R: in a dense net, or when places are replicated, write the place's name inside a smaller circle.*
10. Spacing is uniform in each column; the gap between processes is wide, and the top V is tall, giving the whole figure a tree silhouette. The 21 small circles are mostly copies; the actual process places are about 11.

**What could be better.** Replicating the shared variables is what makes the figure readable, but it also hides the very thing the text asks about. Mutual exclusion fails through interaction via k and b, and with every interaction cut into local copies, nothing in the picture connects the two processes except the t1 fork. The reader cannot see a path from process 0's t7 (sets k0) to process 1's t11 (tests k0) without matching labels. No token dots appear, so the initial marking must be inferred. The tall t4 -> p2 and t10 -> p7 return arcs fill the center gap between the critical sections, partly undoing the pairing of p4 and p9.

## Figure 4.9  (fig-04-09.png, page p-107)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** No system; "a marked Petri net for illustrating the construction of a reachability tree." Initial marking (1, 0, 0); t1 and t2 are enabled initially.

**Structure, briefly.** p1 (marked), p2, p3. t1 is in a self-loop with p1 and outputs to p2 (a generator: each firing adds to p2). t2 moves p1's token to p3 and also outputs to p2. t3 is in a self-loop with p3 and consumes from p2. It is Figure 4.7 without t0 and its output place, and with an extra output t2 -> p2.

**Placement observations.**
1.
