# Chapter 4 figures: placement analysis

## Figure 4.1  (fig-04-01.png, page p-097)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Nothing physical. It is "a simple Petri net" that is not safe, used as the input to the forced-safeness construction of Figure 4.2. Safeness is defined as no place ever holding more than one token.

**Structure, briefly.** A straight chain t1 -> p1 -> t2 -> p2 -> t3. t1 is a source transition (no inputs), t3 a sink. p3 holds one token and forms a self-loop with t2 (input and output). Unsafe because t1 can fire repeatedly and pile tokens into p1.

**Placement observations.**
1. The chain lies on one horizontal line: t1 at the far left, then p1, t2, p2, t3 at the far right, evenly spaced. *Says:* the net is one pipeline, read left to right, with a source at the left end and a sink at the right end. *R: a chain whose ends are a source and a sink is one straight line, source first in reading direction, sink last.*
2. The side place p3 sits directly above t2, off the main line, and is the only marked place. *Says:* p3 is not part of the pipeline; it is a condition t2 consults and restores. Putting it above, perpendicular to the flow, makes "off to the side" literal. *R: a place that only forms a self-loop with one transition sits perpendicular to the main line, directly opposite that transition.*
3. The self-loop is a narrow lens of two curved arcs between p3 and t2, one down, one up, bowing to opposite sides. *Says:* "taken and given back" in one gesture; the symmetry makes the pair read as one relation. *R: a self-loop pair is drawn as two mirror-image curves between the place and the transition, one each way.*
4. Bars are vertical, across the horizontal flow. t1 and t3 are long full-height bars; t2 is a very short tick, almost a point, where four arcs meet. Unclear whether the short t2 is deliberate or an artifact of crowding the arc heads; it does make t2 hard to see as a transition. *R: a bar is perpendicular to the flow; keep it long enough to read as a bar even when many arcs land on it.*
5. All labels sit below their element on the main line (t1, p1, t2, p2, t3 in one row under the line). p3's label is to its upper right. *Says:* the label row repeats the reading order. *R: labels of a horizontal chain go in one row on the same side, the side no arc uses.*
6. Main-line arcs are straight; only the self-loop curves. *R: straight along the flow, curved only off it.*

**What could be better.** t2 is nearly invisible as a bar; the reader infers it from the label. The unsafe place (p1) is not marked out in any way, though the text does not need it to be.

## Figure 4.2  (fig-04-02.png, page p-097)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Figure 4.1 "forced to be safe". Each place p_i to be made safe gets a complement place p_i' that holds a token exactly when p_i does not; transitions that consume from p_i deposit into p_i' and vice versa.

**Structure, briefly.** Figure 4.1 unchanged, plus p1' and p2', each marked. p1' is output of t2 and input of t1; p2' is output of t3 and input of t2. So t1 -> p1 -> t2 -> p1' -> t1 and t2 -> p2 -> t3 -> p2' -> t2 become two small cycles sharing t2. p3 is untouched.

**Placement observations.**
1. The original net keeps its exact arrangement: the same horizontal line t1, p1, t2, p2, t3, with p3 above t2 and the same self-loop lens. *Says:* this is Figure 4.1 with something added; the reader compares the two at a glance and sees only the new elements as new. *R: when a figure is a transformation of an earlier one, keep every original node in its original relative position and add the new nodes in previously empty space.*
2. The new complement places sit below the line, each directly under the gap of the place it complements (p1' under p1, p2' under p2). *Says:* p_i' pairs with p_i; vertical alignment states "this is p_i's partner". Below the line is the side that was empty in 4.1 (opposite p3). *R: a complement place sits directly opposite its partner across the main line, on the side free of other additions.*
3. Each complement arc pair forms a shallow arch under the line: t2 -> p1' -> t1 curves back leftward, t3 -> p2' -> t2 curves back leftward. With the straight forward arcs above, each p_i, p_i' pair reads as a small loop: forward along the top, back along the bottom. *Says:* "the token returns" as a backward flow below the forward one. *R: a return path runs parallel to the forward path, on the opposite side from other attachments, curving back against the flow.*
4. The whole is mirror-like left and right of t2: two identical returns. *Says:* the same construction applied twice. *R: identical constructions applied to two places are drawn as mirror or translated copies.*
5. Labels moved: in 4.1 t1, p1, t2, p2, t3 were labeled below; now the line labels are above (t1, p1, p2, t3) and t2 is below, because the bottom is now taken by arcs and complement places. The new places are labeled below. *Says:* labels yield to arcs. *R: labels go on whichever side is free after arcs and nodes are placed, even if that breaks consistency with a previous figure.*
6. The complements are marked (one token each, placed centered). The token placement again is center of place. *Says:* initial state "p_i empty so p_i' full" is visible.

**What could be better.** t2 is still a near-invisible point, now with six arcs meeting there; the left arc into t2 from p2' and the arc out to p1' meet at the same tick, hard to read which is which. Label sides flipped relative to 4.1, slightly weakening the "same net" comparison.

## Figure 4.3  (fig-04-03.png, page p-099)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Introduced under conservation, which the text motivates by resource allocation ("a set of three line printers is represented by a place with an initial marking of three tokens"). The net is "not strictly conservative since the firing of either transition t1 or t3 will decrease the number of tokens by one, while firing transition t2 or t4 will add a token" (OCR; the parity of the transition names in that sentence looks garbled, but the point is that token count changes). It is conservative with weights (1, 1, 2, 2, 1). The shape is the classic two-process mutual exclusion net, though the text does not name it so here.

**Structure, briefly.** Two cycles: p1 -> t1 -> p3 -> t3 -> p1 and p2 -> t2 -> p4 -> t4 -> p2. p1, p2 marked. Shared place p5, marked, is an input of t1 and t2 and an output of t3 and t4. So t1, t2 are synchronizations (two inputs, one output) and t3, t4 forks (one input, two outputs).

**Placement observations.**
1. Each process is a vertical column: marked place at top, bar, place, bar, top to bottom. Left column p1, t1, p3, t3; right column p2, t2, p4, t4. *Says:* each process is one sequence read downward, starting at its token. *R: a process cycle is cut open at its marked place and laid as a straight column, marked place first.*
2. The two columns are mirror images about the vertical center line, with matching nodes at equal heights. *Says:* the two processes are identical peers; same height means same stage. *R: identical processes are drawn as mirror images, stages aligned across.*
3. The shared place p5 sits on the center line, between the columns, at a height between the acquire bars (t1, t2) and the release bars (t3, t4), closer to the acquire bars. *Says:* p5 belongs to neither process; it is held during the stretch between acquire and release. *R: a resource shared by two processes sits on their axis of symmetry, vertically between the transitions that take it and those that return it.*
4. The acquire arcs leave the top of p5 and arch up and outward into the inner end of t1 and t2. The release arcs leave the inner ends of t3, t4 by a small downward hook, then sweep up and inward into the bottom of p5. The four arcs form an X-like butterfly centered on p5. *Says:* p5 is lent out at the top and comes back from the bottom; the inner side of each column is "the resource side". *R: arcs to and from a shared place attach on the inner end of each bar, the end facing the shared place.*
5. Each cycle's return arc (t3 -> p1, t4 -> p2) leaves the outer end of the bottom bar with a downward hook, swings out wide along the outer edge, and enters the top place from above-outside. *Says:* "and repeat", on the side away from the shared resource. *R: a cycle's return arc runs on the outer side of its column, away from what the process shares, and enters the first place from above.*
6. Bars are horizontal, across a vertical flow. Each bar is drawn longer to the inner side, giving room for the second arc. Labels sit on the outer end (t1, t3 left; t2, t4 right). Place labels are on the outer side too (p1, p3 left; p2, p4 right), p5 above. *R: transition and place labels go on the outer side of a column, the side facing away from the shared center.*
7. Outputs leaving a bar downward to two destinations exit as two separate stubs: the hooks at t3 and t4 descend from the bar a short way before curving. *Says:* each output is a distinct arc, visibly parted at the bar. *R: when a bar has two outputs, they leave from separate points along the bar, ordered by the side their target is on.*
8. Tokens are single centered dots; all three marked places are at the top half of the figure. *Says:* the initial state is "everyone at the start, resource free".

**What could be better.** The return arcs swing very wide and very tall; they dominate the outline of the figure more than the processes do. The weights (1, 1, 2, 2, 1) the text uses are invisible; the drawing gives no hint why p3 and p4 weigh 2.

## Figure 4.4  (fig-04-04.png, page p-099)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "A strictly conservative Petri net which is equivalent to the net of Figure 4.3." Every transition now has as many outputs as inputs.

**Structure, briefly.** Figure 4.3 with each arc t1 -> p3, p3 -> t3, t2 -> p4, p4 -> t4 doubled (multiplicity 2). Nothing else changes.

**Placement observations.**
1. Every node of 4.3 is in the same place: same columns, same p5 position, same return arcs, same butterfly, same labels. *Says:* the reader compares 4.3 and 4.4 and sees the only change. *R: a transformed net keeps every node position and arc route of its original; only the change is new.*
2. Multiplicity 2 is drawn as two parallel straight arcs side by side, not as a number on one arc. The second arc of each pair sits on the inner side (toward p5), and the bars are extended inward to receive it. *Says:* p3 now carries two tokens' worth per pass; the doubled line makes the "weight 2" of p3, p4 visible, which is exactly why 4.3 was conservative with weights (1, 1, 2, 2, 1). Placement turns the hidden weighting into visible thickness. *R: a small multiplicity is drawn as that many parallel arcs, offset toward the free side, with the bars widened to take them.*
3. Because the arcs are parallel and straight, each column now looks like a doubled rail from t1 down to t3. *Says:* the critical section stretch is "heavier", which fits the conservation story: the resource token is inside it. Inference; the text does not say this.
4. The release hooks from t3, t4 into p5 and the outer returns stay attached at the same ends of the bars, so the bars' extra length goes into the middle of the bar. *R: when an element grows to take more arcs, existing attachments keep their side and the new ones go between.*

**What could be better.** Doubling by parallel lines is fine at 2 but would not scale; the book elsewhere uses numbers or multiple arcs inconsistently. The p5 butterfly arcs now nearly touch the inner rails near t1 and t2.
