# Chapter 2 figures: placement analysis

Chapter 2 introduces the notation, so almost every net here is abstract (p1, t2) with no modeled system. The placement question for these is what the drawing makes easy to see: direction, start, grouping, symmetry, and the shape of the structure. Several figures redraw one net. Those comparisons are where the chapter teaches the most about placement.

## Figure 2.4  (fig-02-04.png, page p-027)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Nothing in the world. It is "a Petri net graph equivalent to the structure of Figure 2.1", drawn to show that the tuple and the graph are the same thing. The tuple (p-025): t1 takes p1 and puts into p2, p3, p5; t2 takes p2, p3, p5 and puts back into p5; t3 takes p3 into p4; t4 takes p4 into p2 and p3.

**Structure, briefly.** Five places, four transitions, no marking. p1 is a pure source place. t1 forks to three places. t2 joins three places, and has a self-loop with p5. A cycle p3 to t3 to p4 to t4 back to p3 (and to p2). So: an entry, a fork, a join, and a small side loop on the right that refeeds the fork's outputs.

**Placement observations.**
1. p1 sits alone at the far left on the horizontal midline, with one straight arc to t1. *Says:* this is where the net starts; it has no inputs, so nothing is to its left. *R: a place with no input arcs goes at the start edge of the reading direction.*
2. The net is mirror-symmetric about the horizontal line through p1, t1, p5, t2, t4, p4. p2 is above that line and p3 below, at the same horizontal position. *Says:* p2 and p3 play the same role (both are outputs of t1 and t4, both inputs of t2). *R: places with the same set of input and output transitions are placed as a mirror pair about the axis through those transitions.*
3. t1's three outputs leave its vertical bar from the top, middle, and bottom of the bar: top goes to p2, middle to p5, bottom to p3. The outer two curve out and over to p2 and p3. *Says:* one event fans out to three places, and the fan is spatially ordered (up, straight, down) matching where the targets sit. *R: a transition's output arcs leave the bar at points ordered the same way as their targets are ordered across the flow.*
4. t2 is a vertical bar to the right of p5 on the midline. p2 comes down-left into its top, p3 up-left into its bottom. *Says:* t2 is a join; its inputs converge from both sides of the axis. *R: a join transition sits on the axis between its mirrored inputs, and they enter from either end of the bar.*
5. The p5 and t2 self-loop is two short parallel horizontal arcs, one each way, stacked. *Says:* p5 is read and written back by t2; the pair reads as a back-and-forth, distinct from flow. *R: a self-loop between a place and a transition is two short parallel straight arcs side by side, the place directly beside the bar.*
6. The right half runs right to left. p4 is at the far right on the midline; its arc goes left into t4; t4 feeds back up-left to p2 and down-left to p3. t3 sits on a diagonal straight arc from p3 up-right to p4, and its bar is drawn slanted, perpendicular to that arc. *Says:* p3 to p4 to t4 to p2/p3 is a loop returning to the middle. The rightmost column is where the loop turns around. *R: the turnaround point of a loop is placed at the far edge, and the return arcs re-enter the shared places from the far side.* This is weak as a reading cue, see below.
7. Bars are vertical for the axis transitions (t1, t2, t4), perpendicular to a horizontal flow. t3 is the exception: its bar is tilted to sit perpendicular to its diagonal arc. *R: a bar is perpendicular to the arc(s) running through it; when it sits mid-arc on a diagonal, it tilts with the arc.*
8. Labels: place labels sit outside the circle on the side away from the center of the net (p2 above, p3 below, p1 and p5 below, p4 below). Transition labels sit above the bar (t1, t2, t4), except t3 whose label is below its tilted bar. *R: a label goes outside its node on the side facing away from the net's interior.*
9. Arcs: straight within the midline and on the short diagonals; the long fork arcs from t1 to p2/p3 and the t4 returns are smooth single curves. *R: straight for short adjacent connections; a single smooth curve when an arc must leave a bar end and swing around to an off-axis target.*

**What could be better.** The direction reverses halfway: left half reads left to right, right half right to left, with t3 slipped onto a diagonal. A reader cannot follow a single direction around the p3, p4, t4 loop without tracing arrowheads. The tilted t3 bar also looks like a crossing mark more than a transition.

## Figure 2.5  (fig-02-05.png, page p-028)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Nothing; the graph form of the structure of Figure 2.2. From the tuple: t1 takes p1 into p2, p3; t2 takes p3 into p3, p5, p5; t3 takes p2, p3 into p2, p4; t4 takes p4, p5, p5, p5 into p4; t5 takes p2 into p6.

**Structure, briefly.** Six places, five transitions, no marking. Source p1, fork at t1, sink p6. Three self-loops (p3 with t2, p2 with t3, p4 with t4). A multi-arc path p3, t2, p5 (weight 2), p5 to t4 (weight 3).

**Placement observations.**
1. p1 at left on its own row, straight arc to vertical bar t1. t1 forks up-right to p2 and down-right to p3. *Says:* start at the left, fork immediately. *R: a source place is placed at the start edge, its transition next to it.*
2. Two horizontal rows: top row p2, t5, p6 runs left to right to the sink p6 at the far right; middle row p3, t3, p4 runs left to right. *Says:* each row is one onward path. The top row ends at p6, which has no outputs, at the far right. *R: a chain of place, transition, place is one straight line in the main direction; a sink place goes at the far end.*
3. t3 sits on the middle row between p3 and p4, but its other input p2 sits directly above it. p2 and t3 are joined by a lens: two curves bulging to opposite sides, one down, one up. *Says:* t3 reads p2 and puts it back (self-loop) while moving p3 to p4. The lens shape keeps the two directions apart. *R: a self-loop between nodes that are far apart is drawn as two curves bowed in opposite directions, making a lens.*
4. The bottom half is a symmetric U. t2 is a horizontal bar directly under p3, t4 a horizontal bar directly under p4, and p5 sits at the bottom center between them. The arcs t2 to p5 (two) and p5 to t4 (three) are concentric parallel curves forming an arch. *Says:* p5 is a buffer that couples two things (the p3 side and the p4 side); the bundle of parallel curves reads as weight. *R: multiple arcs between the same pair are drawn as parallel, evenly spaced copies of one curve. A place that couples two side-by-side nodes hangs below and between them.*
5. Bars t2 and t4 are horizontal because the flow into them is vertical (from p3 and p4 above). t1, t3, t5 are vertical in a horizontal flow. *R: bar perpendicular to the arcs entering it.*
6. The self-loops of t2 with p3 and t4 with p4 are two short parallel vertical arcs, like 2.4's p5/t2. *R: same as 2.4 (self-loop as two short parallel arcs).*
7. Labels t2 and t4 sit on the outer ends of their bars (t2 left, t4 right). p3's label is left, p4's right. p2 and p6 above. *R: label on the outer side, away from the net's interior.*

**What could be better.** The lens between p2 and t3 is tall and dominates the center; the self-loop it shows is minor. p2 could sit closer to t3. Also t1's role as a fork is clear, but nothing tells a reader that the bottom U is a separate subsystem versus part of the main flow.

## Figure 2.6  (fig-02-06.png, page p-028)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Nothing; the graph form of the structure of Figure 2.3. t1: p1 to p2, p3. t2: p8 to p1, p7. t3: p2, p5 to p6. t4: p3 to p4. t5: p6, p7 to p9. t6: p4, p9 to p5, p8.

**Structure, briefly.** Nine places, six transitions, no marking. One big cycle: t1 forks to two branches (p2 branch and p3, t4, p4 branch); they join at t6 (via t3, t5, p9 on the upper branch); t6 forks back to p8 and p5, which return to t2 and t3. So a fork at the left, a join at the right, and returns.

**Placement observations.**
1. t1 is a vertical bar at the far left edge; t6 a tall vertical bar at the far right edge. Everything else is between. *Says:* the net is a span from a fork to a join. *R: in a net shaped as fork then join, the fork transition and join transition take the two extreme positions along the main axis.*
2. The forward flow runs left to right on two rows: middle row p2, t3, p6, t5, p9, t6 and bottom row p3, t4, p4, then a diagonal up to t6. The bottom row is perfectly straight and horizontal. *Says:* the two branches are concurrent paths from t1 to t6. *R: concurrent branches between a fork and a join are parallel rows.* The rows are not aligned by stage: the bottom row has only three nodes, so p4 and t4 sit under p6, t5 roughly, not under matching stages.
3. Return arcs from t6 go right to left as long straight diagonals: one up to p8 at the top center, one down-left to p5 below p6. p8 continues left to t2, which feeds p1 (top left) and p7. p1 drops diagonally to t1. *Says:* the top of the drawing is the return path; the cycle reads as a clockwise ring (forward along the bottom/middle, back along the top). *R: a cycle's return path runs along the outer edge opposite the forward flow, in the opposite direction.*
4. Returns are straight, not curved, which makes them look like forward arcs pointing left. Only the arrowheads tell direction.
5. t6 is drawn longer than the other bars to receive two inputs and emit two outputs at distinct points. *R: a bar's length grows with the number of arcs attached, so each arc gets its own attachment point.*
6. Synchronizations: t3 (p2 from left, p5 from lower right), t5 (p7 and p6 from left, converging), t6 (p9 from left, p4 from lower left). Inputs converge on the bar from one side when they come from the same direction (t5), from two sides when one is a return (t3). *R: a join's inputs enter the bar from the upstream side, stacked in the order of their sources.*
7. Labels: place labels above or below the circle, alternating with whatever side is free; transition labels below the bar. *R: transition label at the bar's lower end; place label on the free side.*

**What could be better.** The reading direction is unclear. Nothing is marked, and the left edge holds t1, which is fed by p1 coming from the top-right, so a reader has to hunt for a start. p5 sits in the middle band but belongs to a return path; it looks like a forward node. Placing p5 and p8 together on the return band (top or bottom) would separate forward from back.

## Figure 2.7  (fig-02-07.png, page p-029)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "The dual of the Petri net of Figure 2.4": places and transitions swapped. "The graph structure is maintained, simply interchanging the circles and bars."

**Structure, briefly.** Same graph as 2.4 with node types swapped: t1 (old p1) is a source transition, p1 (old t1) is the fork node, t5/p2 (old p5/t2) the self-loop, t2/t3 (old p2/p3) the mirrored pair, p4 (old t4), t4 (old p4), p3 (old t3).

**Placement observations.**
1. Every node keeps its position from 2.4 except old t3. The midline row t1, p1, t5, p2, p4, t4 matches 2.4's p1, t1, p5, t2, t4, p4 one for one; the mirrored pair t2/t3 sits where p2/p3 sat. *Says:* the text's claim that the graph structure is maintained is shown by keeping the layout fixed. Comparison is done by eye, glyph for glyph. *R: when two figures show related nets (dual, relabeling, remarking), keep every shared node at the same position.*
2. Old t3 was a tilted bar on the diagonal p3 to p4. As a place (p3) it cannot sit on that diagonal: it moves down and right, below p4, with a bend at it. *Says:* a circle needs room a bar does not; the layout bent around it. *R: a bar can sit mid-arc; a place needs its own slot, off the line if necessary.*
3. All bars are now vertical, including t2 and t3, even though their arcs arrive diagonally. In 2.4 the off-axis node t3 was tilted. *R (from the contrast): bars are aligned with the main axis (vertical for a left-to-right net) by default; tilting is the exception for a bar sitting alone mid-arc.*
4. Labels: transition labels above the bar (t1, t5, t2, t4), t3 below; place labels below or to the right (p2 right, since its left is taken by the self-loop). *R: label on the free side.*

**What could be better.** The dual inherits 2.4's reversed right half. Moving p3 off the diagonal makes the right side lopsided; the mirror symmetry of 2.4 is broken at the bottom right.

## Figure 2.8  (fig-02-08.png, page p-031)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Nothing; an exercise asks the reader to "give the Petri net structure corresponding to the Petri net graph of Figure 2.8". It exists to be read back into a tuple.

**Structure, briefly.** Read from the image: three places, four transitions, no marking. t1 has a self-loop with p1 and puts two tokens into p2. t2 takes p1 and three from p2, puts two into p3. t3 takes p3 back to p1. t4 takes p3 and outputs nothing (a sink transition). Shape: a triangle cycle p1, t1, p2, t2, p3, t3, p1 with multi-arcs, plus an exit t4.

**Placement observations.**
1. The three places form a triangle: p1 top left, p2 top right, p3 bottom center. t1 sits between p1 and p2 on the top edge; t2 sits in the middle of the triangle above p3; t3 on the left edge. *Says:* the net is one loop of three states. *R: a cycle of three or more places is drawn as a convex ring, places at the corners, transitions on the edges between them.*
2. Flow goes across the top (p1 to t1 to p2), down the middle into t2, down to p3, then back up the left side through t3 to p1. t2 is pulled inward to the center rather than on the p2 to p3 edge, because it also takes p1. *Says:* t2 joins both top places, so it sits below and between them. *R: a join with inputs from two places sits below and between them, equidistant.*
3. The return t3 is on the left outer edge, as one long curve from p3 bowed outward, with t3 a horizontal bar crossing that curve. *R: a return arc runs on the outside of the ring; a transition on it sits mid-arc with the bar across the arc.*
4. Multi-arcs: t1 to p2 is two parallel straight horizontal arcs; p2 to t2 three parallel S-curves in a ribbon; t2 to p3 two parallel vertical arcs. *Says:* weight is shown as a count of parallel strokes, and the ribbon keeps them visibly one bundle. *R: multiple arcs between the same pair run as an evenly spaced parallel ribbon along one path, attaching at adjacent points on the bar.*
5. The self-loop p1 and t1 is a lens: one arc bowed up (t1 to p1), one bowed down (p1 to t1). *R: a self-loop is two arcs bowed apart, one per direction.*
6. t4, the exit, is to the right of p3 on a short straight horizontal arc, outside the triangle. *Says:* it leaves the loop. *R: an exit (sink) transition goes outside the ring, next to the place it drains.*
7. Bars: t1 vertical (flow horizontal), t2 and t3 horizontal (flow vertical). Labels on the outer side: p1 left, p2 right, p3 below, t3 left, t2 right, t1 above.

**What could be better.** t2's horizontal bar is wide so it can take one plus three arcs; it looks like a shelf. t4 has no label cue that it is a sink; that is fine for an exercise. The drawing has no obvious start point since nothing is marked.

## Figure 2.9  (fig-02-09.png, page p-032)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Nothing; a companion exercise ("Give the Petri net structure for Figure 2.9").

**Structure, briefly.** Four places, five transitions, no marking. A three-place cycle p1, t1, p3, t2, p2, t3, p1. t4 reads p3 with a self-loop and puts into p4; t5 drains p4 and outputs nothing.

**Placement observations.**
1. The cycle is an equilateral triangle: p1 bottom left, p2 bottom right, p3 apex. Transitions sit at the midpoints of the edges. Direction: up the left edge, down the right, leftward along the bottom (clockwise). *Says:* three states in a ring. *R: a pure cycle of n places is a regular polygon with places at the vertices and transitions at edge midpoints.*
2. t1 and t2 are tilted bars, each perpendicular to its diagonal edge; t3 is vertical on the horizontal bottom edge. *R: a transition on an edge has its bar perpendicular to that edge.* This matches 2.4's t3.
3. A tower rises straight up from the apex p3: t4, p4, t5 in one vertical column. *Says:* a branch that leaves the cycle and goes nowhere; the column makes it a separate chain. *R: a chain leaving a cycle continues outward from the cycle's node, straight, away from the ring's center.*
4. The self-loop p3 and t4 is two short parallel vertical arcs. Same form as 2.4 and 2.5.
5. Labels: places outside (p4, p3 to the right; p1, p2 below), transitions on the outer side of bars (t5, t4 right; t1 upper left; t2 upper right; t3 below).

**What could be better.** The cycle's direction is not signaled by anything but arrowheads; with no marking there is no start. The tower makes the figure tall and narrow; otherwise clean.

## Figure 2.11  (fig-02-11.png, page p-033)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Introduces tokens: "tokens are represented by small dots in the circles". "The Petri net structure is the same as Figures 2.1 and 2.4. The marking is (1, 2, 0, 0, 1)."

**Structure, briefly.** Claimed to be 2.4's net, with p1 = 1, p2 = 2, p5 = 1. But the drawing is not 2.4's structure: t1 has only one output arc, to p5. The two arcs from t1 to p2 and to p3 that 2.4 shows are missing. I checked this against the clip; the curves from t1 are absent. The figure contradicts its caption.

**Placement observations.**
1. Node positions match 2.4: p1, t1, p5, t2 on the left part of the midline; p2 above and p3 below the center; t4 and p4 on the right; t3 tilted on the p3 to p4 diagonal. The figure is compressed horizontally slightly (p2 sits closer to t4). *Says:* same net, now with tokens. A reader recognizes it as 2.4 by shape alone. *R: a net redrawn to show a different marking keeps its layout; only tokens change.*
2. Because t1's fan arcs are gone, t1 now looks like a plain step in a chain p1, t1, p5, and p5 looks like the net's second state. The mirror pair p2/p3 loses its visible link to t1. This shows how much of 2.4's reading came from those two curves.
3. Tokens: a single token is one dot dead center (p1, p5). Two tokens are side by side horizontally, centered (p2). *R: k tokens are drawn as k dots arranged symmetrically about the place's center; one token is at the center.*
4. Labels are as in 2.4 except t1's label is now above a bar with no arcs at its ends.

**What could be better.** It is wrong: two arcs are missing, so the "same structure" claim fails, and a reader comparing to 2.4 will be confused. As a layout lesson it shows that leaving positions fixed makes such an error visible.

## Figure 2.12  (fig-02-12.png, page p-034)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "The structure is the same as the structure of Figure 2.11, but the marking is different." From the dots: p2 = 2, p4 = 4, p5 = 1, others 0.

**Structure, briefly.** 2.4's net, this time with all of 2.4's arcs (t1 to p2 and p3 present). Marking (0, 2, 0, 4, 1).

**Placement observations.**
1. Layout is 2.4's, node for node, including the t1 fan curves and the tilted t3. Comparing with 2.11 shows that 2.11, not 2.12, deviated. *R: as in 2.11: the marking changes, the layout does not.*
2. Tokens: p2's two tokens are on a diagonal (upper left, lower right) here, not side by side as in 2.11. p4's four tokens form a loose 2-by-2 cluster around the center. p5 one centered dot. *Says:* the count is what matters; the arrangement is free. *R: tokens fill the circle in a compact, roughly symmetric cluster; their arrangement carries no meaning.* The inconsistency between 2.11 and 2.12 for two tokens suggests no fixed rule was followed.
3. Places are drawn the same size whether they hold 0 or 4 tokens. *R: place size is constant; token count never resizes a place.*

**What could be better.** The p2 and p3 positions are slightly different from 2.4 (p3 sits a bit further right). Minor drift between redrawings; an engine should be exact here.

## Figure 2.13  (fig-02-13.png, page p-034)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** The convention for large markings: when a place holds many tokens, "write the number of tokens in the place". Marking (47, 13, 7, 42).

**Structure, briefly.** Read from the image, four places, three transitions. t1 takes one from p1, gives two back to p1 and one to p3. t2 takes p1 into p2. t3 takes three from p3 and one from p4, gives one to p2 and two to p4. All places marked.

**Placement observations.**
1. A triangle again: p1 top left, p2 top right, p3 bottom center; p4 off to the lower right of the triangle. t2 is mid-edge on the top (vertical bar, horizontal flow), t1 mid-edge on the left diagonal, t3 mid-edge on the right diagonal. *R: as 2.9 (transitions at edge midpoints of a polygon).*
2. t1 and t3 are tilted bars perpendicular to their diagonal edges, long enough to catch three parallel arcs each. *R: as 2.4 and 2.9 (bar perpendicular to the edge it sits on).*
3. The multi-arcs on the diagonals are parallel straight strokes. On p1 and t1, the three strokes mix directions: two up into p1, one down from p1 that continues through t1 on to p3. That continuation is drawn as a single straight line through the bar, so the p1 to t1 to p3 path reads straight through. *R: when a transition's input and output lie on opposite sides of the bar on one line, the two arcs are collinear, passing straight through the bar.*
4. p4 is outside the triangle with its exchange with t3 drawn as a large loop: two outputs from t3 arch over the top into p4, one input from p4 swings round below and up into t3. *Says:* p4 is a side store attached to t3, not part of the triangle. *R: a place that only exchanges with one transition hangs off that transition, outside the main shape, its arcs forming a closed loop.*
5. Token counts are numerals centered in the circle. Places stay the same size. *R: above a few tokens, the count is a numeral at the place's center.*
6. Labels: p1, t2, p2 above; t1 lower left; t3 upper left; p3, p4 below. Outer side again.

**What could be better.** The p4 loop is large and bulbous, larger than the triangle edges, and the t3 bar is crowded where five arcs meet. The output arc t3 to p2 starts at the middle of three input strokes, which is hard to separate. An engine should give each arc its own attachment point on the bar, inputs on one side, outputs on the other.

## Figure 2.14  (fig-02-14.png, page p-036)

**Kind.** net fragment (a 2-by-2 table of fragments)

**What the text says it models.** "How the marking of a place changes when a transition tj fires. Each place may or may not be an input or output of the transition." Rows: pi is not / is an input of tj. Columns: pi is not / is an output.

**Structure, briefly.** Four cells, each one place pi and one transition tj. Cell contents: no arc; output arc only; input arc only; both (a self-loop).

**Placement observations.**
1. In every cell tj is a vertical bar at top center and pi a circle at bottom center, at identical positions. Only the arcs differ. *Says:* the cells are one situation with one variable changed; fixed positions make the arc the only thing to compare. *R: in a set of variants of one fragment, node positions are identical across variants.*
2. The input arc always swings around the left side (pi up the left into the bar's left face); the output arc always swings around the right side (bar's right face down into pi). In the self-loop cell both are present and together form a full circle, left half in, right half out. *Says:* in flows from the left, out goes to the right, even when the place is both. *R: input arcs attach to the upstream face of a bar and output arcs to the downstream face; for a self-loop, the two arcs bow out to opposite sides so the loop reads as a circulation.*
3. Here the self-loop is a wide round loop, whereas in 2.4, 2.5, 2.9 it was two short parallel straight arcs. The circle form appears when the place sits off the flow axis (below the bar); the parallel form when the place sits on the axis beside the bar. *R: self-loop form depends on relative position; place beside the bar on the flow axis gives two parallel arcs, place across the flow gives a two-sided loop.*
4. Labels: tj to the right of the bar's top; pi inside the circle (unusual, since there are no tokens to show); formula below the place.

**What could be better.** Nothing major. Putting pi's label inside the circle is a one-off that conflicts with the token convention.

## Figure 2.15  (fig-02-15.png, page p-037)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "A marked Petri net to illustrate the firing rules. Transitions t1, t3, and t4 are enabled." The text then fires t4, then t1, then t3, giving Figures 2.16, 2.17, 2.18.

**Structure, briefly.** This is 2.4's net shape relabeled, with weights added: t1 takes p1 into p2, p3, and two into p4; t2 takes p2, p3, p4 and returns to p2 (self-loop); t3 takes two from p4 into p5; t4 takes p5 into p3, p4. So p2 here is 2.4's p5, p3 is 2.4's p2, p4 is 2.4's p3, p5 is 2.4's p4. Marking: p1 = 1, p4 = 2, p5 = 1.

**Placement observations.**
1. The layout is 2.4's: p1 left on the midline, t1 vertical bar, p2 (self-loop place) next, t2, then the diamond with p3 at top and p4 at bottom, t4 and p5 on the right, t3 tilted between p4 and p5. Peterson reused the geometry of an earlier figure for a new net, renaming nodes. *Says:* a reader who studied 2.4 sees the familiar shape. *R: across a sequence of figures, a net that shares a shape with an earlier one reuses its geometry.*
2. Compared with 2.4 the diamond is taller and wider: p3 rises higher, p4 sinks lower, and the t1 fan arcs and t4 return arcs are rounder. t3 now sits on a curved arc rising from p4 to p5 rather than a straight diagonal. This is a cleaner version: the curved right side reads as one swing from bottom to the right edge.
3. The weight-2 arcs (t1 to p4 and p4 to t3) are two parallel curves of equal shape, like a double rail. *R: as 2.5, 2.8 (multi-arcs as parallel copies).*
4. Labels for p3 and p5 are to the right of the place and p1, p2, p4 to the lower right, unlike 2.4 where they sat above or below. Transition labels are below bars here (t1, t2, t4), above in 2.4. *Says:* label side is not a fixed rule in Peterson; it goes where there is room.
5. Tokens: single dots centered; two tokens on a diagonal.

**What could be better.** Same issue as 2.4: the right half runs right to left, so the eye has to reverse. Nothing marks which part is the "main" path.

## Figure 2.16  (fig-02-16.png, page p-037)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** "The marking resulting from firing transition t4 in Figure 2.15." Firing t4 "removes the token in p5, deposits one token in p3, and increases the number of tokens in p4 from two to three."

**Structure, briefly.** Same net as 2.15. Marking p1 = 1, p3 = 1, p4 = 3.

**Placement observations.**
1. Every node, arc, and label is in the same place as 2.15 (by eye they appear to be the same drawing). ## Figure 2.19  (fig-02-19.png, page p-038)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** Nothing; an exercise: "Can transitions be fired in Figure 2.19? Which?"

**Structure, briefly.** Read from the image: six places, four transitions. t1 takes p6, p2, p3 into p1. t2 takes p6 into p5. t3 takes p5, p2, p3 and puts two into p4. t4 takes p4 into p3. Marking p1 = 3, p3 = 2, p5 = 1. p2 and p3 each feed both t1 and t3 (conflict). With this marking nothing is enabled, which is presumably the point of the exercise.

**Placement observations.**
1. Two transitions at opposite ends of the midline: t1 at left, t3 at right. Between them, a column of three places p6 (top), p2 (middle), p3 (bottom), all at about the same horizontal position, closer to t1. p2 sits on the midline with one straight arc going left to t1 and one going right to t3. *Says:* p2 and p3 are each contested by t1 and t3: they feed both directions. The column between the two competitors reads as shared inputs. *R: places in conflict between two transitions sit between those transitions, with the transitions on opposite sides.*
2. Flow runs both ways from the center: leftward to t1 and the sink p1 at the far left; rightward to t3. *Says:* two alternative outcomes leave the shared pool in opposite directions. *R: alternative consumers of the same places go on opposite sides of them.* This is unusual and reads as a choice rather than a sequence.
3. p6 feeds t1 (down-left) and t2 (right, along the top row). t2 and p5 make a top row p6, t2, p5, with p5 then descending to t3. So the top row is a path from the shared column to t3.
4. The return t4 runs along the bottom, right to left: t3 outputs two arcs that curve down to p4 at bottom right, then p4, t4, p3 is a straight horizontal line back. *Says:* the loop t3, p4, t4, p3, t3 closes along the bottom edge. *R: a return path runs along an outer edge, straight, opposite to the forward direction.*
5. p1, the sink, is at the far left on the midline. *R: a sink place at the end edge of its flow.*
6. Weight-2 output from t3 to p4 is two concentric arcs swinging down the right side. *R: as before.*
7. Bars all vertical. Labels: places below, except p6 and p5 above; transitions below except t2 above. Free side.
8. Tokens: three as a triangle, two diagonal.

**What could be better.** The long arc from p3 up to t3 crosses the drawing diagonally and is hard to separate from p2's straight arc into t3. Because flow goes both left and right, a reader has no single direction; that does fit a two-way choice, but the drawing does not signal that t1 and t3 compete. Placing p2 and p3 symmetrically between t1 and t3 (centered) would make the contest clearer.

## Figure 2.22  (fig-02-22.png, page p-043)

**Kind.** net

**What the text says it models.** A board for a Petri net board game the reader is asked to design: "Use the Petri net of Figure 2.22 as a playing board." Tokens are pieces; players take turns firing enabled transitions.

**Structure, briefly.** Thirteen places, thirteen transitions labeled with letters a to n (no l, as far as I can see), no marking. Dense: several joins (f has p3, p4, p5, p6 as inputs; i has inputs p8, p9, p10 and outputs p5, p6, p8), self-loops (p8 with e, p13 with k, p8 with i), and long arcs spanning the board. I did not trace every arc; some arrowheads at crowded bars (c, d, j) are ambiguous at this resolution.

**Placement observations.**
1. No single flow direction. Arcs run in every direction and the board is filled roughly evenly inside an oval. *Says:* this is a playing surface, not a process; there is no start. That fits the purpose, a board to wander, but it makes the net hard to grasp as structure.
2. A central vertical spine: f at top center, p5 and p6 below it as a mirror pair, i below them as a tall vertical bar. f collects p5 and p6; i emits to p5 and p6. The p5, p6 pair sits symmetrically between f and i, forming a diamond. *Says:* p5 and p6 play the same role, and the spine is the hub of the board. *R: as 2.4 (places with the same inputs and outputs are a mirror pair about the axis through their transitions).*
3. Long perimeter arcs: p1 down the far left to p11; b around the far right to p7; n along the bottom to p7. *Says:* long-distance connections go around the outside instead of through the dense middle. *R: an arc between distant nodes routes along the outer boundary of the drawing, not across the interior.*
4. Bars take many orientations: vertical (a, b, c, f, i, n), horizontal (j, k), and tilted (d, e, g, h, m). Tilted bars sit on diagonal arcs, perpendicular to them, as in 2.4 and 2.9. With many tilts the bars stop reading as one kind of thing.
5. Self-loops: p13 and k are two parallel vertical arcs (bar above the place); p8 and e form a tight lens loop; p8 and i two long near-parallel arcs. All three forms seen earlier in the chapter appear on one drawing.
6. Labels: transition letters at a bar end on the free side; place labels inside the circles (p1 to p13 written inside), unlike every other figure here, presumably because tokens would be plastic pieces placed on top.

**What could be better.** Hard to read as a net. Nothing is aligned in rows or columns beyond the central spine, arcs cross in the middle (i to p8 and p9, e to p5), and arrowheads near c and d are crowded. It is a deliberately tangled board, so this may be by design, but it is the least instructive figure in the chapter for layout.
